## In the world

### Lexington, Kentucky voters make citizens' assemblies a permanent part of the city charter

New America reports that on 3 November, Lexington voters approved all three recommendations of the city's first civic assembly, "including making assemblies a permanent part of the city's charter" ([New America, 4 Nov 2026](https://www.newamerica.org/insights/a-look-into-lexingtons-first-civic-assembly/)). The three proposals covered councilmember pay, public attendance and accountability expectations for the council, and a charter review every eight years by a deliberative body of residents.

Why it matters: the usual weakness of a one-off assembly is that governments can ignore it. Here an assembly's recommendations went to a public ballot, and the vote locked in the mechanism for future assemblies. That is a step from a project towards an institution, which fits the scope axiom well: citizens helping shape the rules they are governed by. One caveat: this rests on New America's account, which draws on interviews with the organisers (CivicLex), and I could not confirm vote totals or the exact ballot wording.

### Victoria drops group voting tickets before its 28 November election

The Victorian Parliament passed the Electoral Amendment (Miscellaneous Matters) Bill on 11 August, and the Electoral Commission states that "Group voting tickets have been removed from Legislative Council elections" ([VEC](https://www.vec.vic.gov.au/about-us/legislation/electoral-act-amendments-2026)). Voters must now number at least five boxes, above or below the line, and preferences flow in the order candidates appear on the party's list ([Premier's release, 29 July 2026](https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/making-victorias-elections-fairer-and-more-transparent), written before passage).

Why it matters: under group voting tickets, parties decided where a voter's preferences went, which is how tiny parties won upper-house seats on a sliver of first preferences. Moving to voter-directed preferences puts that decision back with the voter, which is the direction the framework's "for and with the people" test points. The first election run under the new rules is this Saturday, so the effect on the upper-house result will be visible soon.
