## In the world

**Ukraine's first metropolitan citizens' assembly concludes in Lviv with 19 recommendations.** The Lviv Agglomeration assembly — convened across 13 communities on "how to preserve and use water resources sustainably"[^lviv1] — ran its opening session 13–14 June 2026, with further sessions on 27–28 June and 11–12 July 2026.[^lviv1] Over the three sessions, "local residents collaborated with experts to co-design 19 practical recommendations, which have been delivered to the local authorities of the Agglomeration's 13 communities,"[^lviv2] covering joint water-management frameworks, sewerage infrastructure, ecological protection, and public-awareness campaigns; local leaders "provided positive feedback and began outlining possible first steps for implementation."[^lviv2] It's organised by the Council of Europe's Congress of Local and Regional Authorities alongside Kyiv and Rivne assemblies running in parallel. This extends the deliberative-democracy thread already in the landscape — wartime Ukraine ran a sortition-based assembly at inter-municipal scale through to a concrete set of recommendations, not just a single-city pilot, while under active invasion.

**V-Dem's 2026 Democracy Report finds nearly a quarter of the world's nations autocratizing, with six of ten new entrants in Europe and North America.** "Nearly a quarter of the world’s nations are going through democratic backsliding, or autocratization, in 2025, and six out of the ten new autocratizing countries identified in the 2026 Democracy Report are in Europe and North America."[^vdem] The press release names three of the ten explicitly — "large and influential countries like Italy, the United Kingdom, and the USA"[^vdem] — and states "rule of law is deteriorating in 22 countries, including the USA"[^vdem]. "Democracy in the USA is deteriorating at unprecedented speed"[^vdem]. These are structural, not electoral, measures — whether avenues for dissent and oversight stay open between elections — which is exactly the kind of question the accountability framework's structural-inflexibility test asks of every entry in this landscape, liberal democracies included. The framework says treat every system by the same yardstick — this is that yardstick turned on the wealthiest democracies in the sample, not just the usual subjects.

**A different index reached the opposite headline the same year.** The Economist Intelligence Unit's Democracy Index, in the full report it released on 7 April 2026, found "the average score in the 2025 Democracy Index rose less than a half of a percent from 5.17 to 5.19"[^eiu], classifying 26 countries as full democracies, 48 as flawed, 32 as hybrid regimes, and 61 as authoritarian across 167 countries and territories.[^eiu] That's a genuinely different top-line story from V-Dem's record-backsliding framing above — yet on the one country both indices call out by name, they agree: "The US fell six spots in the global ranking to 34th, slipping further into the "flawed democracy" category, where it has stood since 2016."[^eiu] its score falling from 7.85 to 7.65,[^eiu] attributed to the same functioning-of-government and civil-liberties weaknesses V-Dem flags. Two indices measuring democracy differently can both be right at once; citing only the one with the more dramatic headline would be the kind of selective framing the framework asks us not to practice on ourselves.

**Connecticut's first state-level citizens' assembly convened its opening session on schedule.** The Connecticut Lottery Corporation drew members in a public lottery on 12 June 2026;[^ct-jul-a] the assembly's own site describes it as selecting "100 ordinary citizens,"[^ct-about] though CT Mirror's coverage of the opening session counted "98 Connecticut residents" in the room that day.[^ct-jul-b] It opened with a two-day in-person session at Yale on 11–12 July, the first of six sessions running through 26 September, split between in-person days in New Haven and virtual sessions[^ct-jul-c] — not Hartford, which doesn't feature in either source for this item — to develop recommendations on how the state funds local public services. It's convened jointly by the State Comptroller's office, Yale, and UConn, empowered to "pass recommendations directly to a legislative hearing."[^ct-about] It's a useful contrast to the Ukraine and EU assemblies above: a citizens' assembly convened by elected officials, inside an established liberal democracy, on a genuinely difficult distributive question ordinary electoral politics has struggled to resolve — exactly the kind of case for sortition-based deliberation that doesn't depend on a system being in crisis or being non-Western to be worth doing.

**DemocracyNext published lessons from four years advising cities and regions on institutionalizing citizens' assemblies.** *From Projects to Permanence*, released 15 July 2026, draws on DemocracyNext's Cities Programme work with Esch-sur-Alzette (Luxembourg), Vilnius, Kerewan (The Gambia), and Central Oregon to argue "establishing an ongoing citizens’ assembly while building supportive civic infrastructure requires a completely different mindset than implementing a one-off assembly"[^demnext] — its central line is to treat the work "like a marathon, not a sprint."[^demnext] DemocracyNext is already tracked in this landscape; this is exactly the kind of institutional-design question the accountability framework's structural-inflexibility test cares about from the other direction — a citizens' assembly that exists for one cycle and then evaporates is no more structurally resilient than any other reform that depends on one administration's goodwill to survive the next.

**Kazakhstan's new constitution took effect on 1 July 2026, replacing the 1995 document.** "Kazakhstan's new Constitution entered into force on July 1 following its approval in a nationwide referendum on March 15."[^kz-astana] It "replac[es] the Mazhilis and Senate with a unicameral parliament, the Kurultai, consisting of 145 deputies,"[^kz-astana] restores "the office of vice president, which was abolished in 1996,"[^kz-astana] and establishes a 126-member Halyk Kenesi ("People's Council") — 42 seats each for ethnocultural associations, major public associations, and regional public councils — with "the right to submit legislative proposals, initiate national referendums, organize public consultations and participate in public oversight."[^kz-astana] But the People's Council's entire membership is appointed rather than elected, and the same package gives the president power to dissolve the Kurultai over repeated nominee rejections, rule by decree while it's dissolved, and appoint key officials without parliamentary approval. Political analyst Dimash Alzhanov, writing in ConstitutionNet's "Voices from the Field" section on the January draft — before the March referendum, not the adopted text — argued "the draft revealed that the parliamentary reform was largely a façade,"[^kz-cn] titling the piece "Parliamentary Reform as a Façade for Presidential Monopoly." A wholly appointed consultative body that can propose but not check power is the same "bounded articulation" pattern the framework already uses to describe China's CPPCC — worth naming here rather than reserving the label for one country.

**Israel's Knesset completed its full four-year term for the first time since 1988 — after a contested last-minute legislative blitz.** "It is a rare historical milestone, marking the first time since 1988 that a Knesset has completed its legally mandated, full four-year term."[^il-aj] Parliament dissolved on 17 July 2026 ahead of an election set for 27 October. But in the nights immediately before dissolution, Netanyahu's coalition passed a cluster of bills to secure ultra-Orthodox coalition partners' support: the "legislative blitz" included a sweeping Communications Law (passed 53-48), which dismantles independent media regulators in favour of a government-supervised broadcast authority,[^il-aj] a Basic Law on Torah Study and draft-evasion immunity shielding ultra-Orthodox men from military service, and "legislation splitting the role of the attorney general"[^il-yn]. Separately, "a bill establishing a political commission of inquiry into Hamas' Oct. 7, 2023, attack... passed a preliminary reading but is not expected to advance further before the election"[^il-yn] — stalled rather than outright blocked. Attorney Tomer Naor put it in exactly the framework's own terms at the protests: "Fifty-eight coalition lawmakers voted for legislation that creates two classes before the law — those subject to the rule of law and those who are not."[^il-yn] Media-regulator capture and selective legal accountability are precisely the hypocrisy and structural-inflexibility patterns the accountability framework asks to be named consistently — including, as the framework insists, in a system DOD's own membership is culturally close to. Israel Democracy Institute and Movement for Quality Government in Israel, both already tracked in this landscape and both reverified this run, are the two organisations most directly positioned to hold this to account domestically.

**AANES's civilian institutions — including TEV-DEM, tracked in this landscape — are being absorbed into the Syrian state under the terms of a January 2026 integration deal with Damascus.** Following a Syrian government military offensive against SDF-held territory that expanded through mid-January 2026, "the two sides announced a comprehensive agreement on January 30 to stabilize the ceasefire and launch phased integration."[^aanes-mec] "Militarily, select SDF units are reorganized under Syrian command, while heavy weapons, borders, crossings, oil fields and main roads transfer to state control,"[^aanes-mec] alongside Kurdish-rights guarantees: al-Sharaa's "Decree No. 13 (2026), granting citizenship to stateless Kurds, recognizing Kurdish as a “national language,” and designating Newroz... as a national holiday."[^aanes-mec] The Council's own analysis is blunt about what this means for the AANES governance model tracked on TEV-DEM's page: "the Kurdish political project in Syria is not ending so much as being forcibly narrowed."[^aanes-mec] "Kurdish self-rule is being compressed into local administrative competences, localized policing arrangements and cultural guarantees — spaces that remain contested, but not illusory."[^aanes-mec] Implementation remains provisional and contested, but the direction is toward the commune-council structure being folded into, not preserved alongside, the central state. This isn't backsliding in the V-Dem sense above — nobody claimed AANES was a stable liberal democracy — but it is exactly the kind of structural transition the framework says DOD should track rather than look away from once a governance experiment stops being convenient to cite: the commune-council system was included in this landscape as a live experiment in non-statist democratic confederalism, and that experiment's institutional basis is now materially different from how the page has described it until this run's update.

**Senegal's National Assembly curbed the president's power to dissolve parliament — while riot police tear-gassed protesters opposing the reform itself.** The amendment, adopted 29 June 2026, "strengthens parliament's powers, such as requiring the government to inform the legislature of agreements related to the exploitation of natural resources," and "expands the powers of parliamentary inquiry committees."[^sn] "The text also proposes the creation of a Constitutional Court to replace the current Constitutional Council. The new court would be composed of nine members, compared to the current seven."[^sn] Other changes include "the incompatibility of the functions of head of state and leader of a political party... and stricter controls on the president's power to dissolve the National Assembly."[^sn] On its face this is exactly the kind of check the accountability framework's structural-inflexibility test asks for — a legislature clawing back a lever an executive could otherwise use against it. But the framework also asks about process, not just text: the reform followed President Faye's May dismissal of Ousmane Sonko as prime minister and Sonko's subsequent election as Assembly president, "the opposition views the initiative... as political revenge by the former prime minister, who retains significant influence over the parliamentary majority,"[^sn] and police met protesters chanting "Hands off my Constitution!" with tear gas and detained several opposition leaders and activists.[^sn] A government still has to submit the amendment to a referendum before it takes effect. Genuine structural improvement and bad-faith factional maneuvering aren't mutually exclusive — the framework's good-faith test and its structural-inflexibility test can point in different directions on the same amendment, and this is a case where they do, at least until the referendum settles whether it sticks.
