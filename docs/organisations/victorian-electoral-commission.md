The Victorian Electoral Commission (VEC) is the state's electoral authority — "an independent and impartial body established under Victoria's *Electoral Act 2002*."[^about] It conducts Victorian state elections, local council elections, and a range of other statutory and commercial ballots, and also acts as a regulator of electoral conduct.

It sits in the Democracy Landscape as electoral infrastructure rather than as an advocacy body: the institution that actually administers the vote, and whose own conduct determines whether the process can be trusted.

## Leadership

**Sven Bluemmel** has been Electoral Commissioner since August 2023.[^bluemmel] He came to the role from the information-access side of government rather than from electoral administration — he was the inaugural Victorian Information Commissioner and before that Western Australian Information Commissioner, and practised information and privacy law in Melbourne and Perth.[^bluemmel]

## Strategy 2027

The VEC launched a four-year strategy in August 2023, framed around navigating a changing environment.[^strategy] Its stated purpose is "Building a stronger future for Victorian democracy," its vision "All Victorians actively participating in their democracy," and its mission "Delivering a trusted democratic process through excellence and impartiality."[^purpose] Trust is the explicit organising metric: "We put electoral integrity at the heart of all our work."[^integrity]

## Misinformation and truth in political advertising

The VEC maintains a public-facing programme on electoral misinformation — including a misinformation register, fact-checking guidance for voters, and partnership work to issue corrections.[^misinfo]

Notably, it states plainly the limit of its own remit here: "There are no laws in Victoria that regulate truth in political advertising."[^truth] What the law does require is authorisation — campaign material must carry the authoriser's name and address "so everyone knows the origins of the electoral campaign material."[^auth] The Commission has extended the same expectation to synthetic media, asking that campaigners using generative AI "carry out their role of informing voters in a responsible and transparent manner by properly authorising all electoral campaign material."[^genai]

That gap — an electoral authority responsible for trust in the process, without any power over the truthfulness of the advertising shaping it — is the structural tension worth watching through the [28 November 2026 state election](../calendar.md).
