The Helen Clark Foundation is an independent public policy think tank based at Auckland University of Technology (AUT). Its research spans four streams: public policy, governance and the future of democracy; sustainability and climate change; inclusive economic development; and a fourth focusing on social wellbeing. The governance and democracy stream positions the Foundation as New Zealand's most prominent institutional voice on democratic reform — distinct from the transparency-and-integrity focus of TINZ and the youth-voting-rights focus of Make It 16.

The Foundation's June 2026 publication "Facing Up to Our Future" — a 20-chapter book challenging New Zealand ahead of the general election — signals an institution willing to frame democratic questions broadly: not just electoral rules or transparency mechanisms, but the structural long-term challenges that representative democracy struggles to address (fiscal sustainability, climate adaptation, intergenerational equity). The Foundation runs an active events programme and newsletter, connecting policy research to public conversation.

Named after Helen Clark, New Zealand's former Prime Minister and UN Development Programme Administrator, the Foundation inherits a tradition of internationally-minded, institutionally-grounded democratic reform advocacy — the kind embodied by Clark's own leadership of the UN's democratic governance work.
