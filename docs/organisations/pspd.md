People's Solidarity for Participatory Democracy (PSPD, 참여연대) was founded in 1994 and has grown into one of South Korea's most influential civil society institutions, with approximately 13,000 members and UN ECOSOC consultative status. The organisation describes its mission as monitoring power (legislative, judicial, executive), proposing alternatives, and organising participation and solidarity — treating watchdog, think-tank, and movement-building functions as interdependent rather than separate activities.

PSPD operates through dedicated centres: judicial monitoring (tracking judicial appointments, prosecutorial accountability, publishing a database of cases and disciplinary records), legislative watchdog work (monitoring the National Assembly), public interest law, administrative oversight, economic and tax reform, labour and social welfare, peace and disarmament, and international solidarity. In August 2026, PSPD launched the "Civil Society Joint Action for Strengthening Accountability and Public Interest in AI" — an example of the organisation extending its accountability framework to emerging technological governance challenges.

PSPD is funded entirely by member dues (no government funding), with over 25,000 members — an independence model that gives it unusual credibility in a political environment where government-funded NGOs face questions about co-optation.

South Korea's broader participatory infrastructure — mandatory participatory budgeting, national deliberative assemblies, and large-scale digital petitions — makes it one of the world's densest participatory governance ecosystems. PSPD is a central node in that ecosystem: not a deliberative practitioner in the narrow sense (it doesn't run citizens' assemblies), but the accountability infrastructure that makes deliberative governance meaningful rather than performative.
