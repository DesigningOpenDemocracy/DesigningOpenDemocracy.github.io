Mzalendo — *patriot* in Swahili — is a Kenyan parliamentary monitoring organisation (PMO) founded in 2005 and run by Mzalendo Trust from Nairobi.[^mz-about] It exists to make the work of Kenya's National Assembly and Senate legible to the people those chambers represent: what each member said, how they voted, which committees they sat on, and what happened to the constituency development funds allocated in their name.

Its mandate is explicitly anchored in Kenya's 2010 Constitution rather than in a generic transparency agenda. The organisation cites Article 118(1) — Parliament "shall conduct its business in an open manner" and "facilitate public participation and involvement in the legislative and other business of Parliament and its committees" — as the constitutional basis for what it does, and organises its programme around three themes drawn from that text: openness, inclusion, and accountability.[^mz-const]

## What it runs

- **MP and senator profiles** — biodata, contributions, motions, bills, statements and petitions for every elected representative, plus voting records and performance rankings
- **A searchable Hansard** and committee-report archive
- **Bonga na Mzalendo** — a structured channel for citizens to submit views on bills, which Parliament's own public-participation process often makes hard to use[^mz-bonga]
- **Annual Parliamentary Scorecard** — a yearly assessment of the sitting Parliament's performance
- **Elections Watch** — gazetted results for every seat, each row linked to the Kenya Gazette notice it came from, currently covering 2022 and building toward 2027
- **Public Finance Observatory** — constituency-level allocations and project tracking

The organisation's stated ambition is regional rather than national: its mission is to "promote open, inclusive, and accountable Parliaments in Kenya and Africa," and it explicitly counts sharing methods with other parliamentary monitoring organisations in the region among its flagship activities.[^mz-mission]

## Why it belongs in the landscape

Mzalendo is the Kenyan member of a small international family of parliamentary monitoring organisations that this landscape already tracks in other countries — [They Vote For You](they-vote-for-you.md) and the [OpenAustralia Foundation](open-australia-foundation.md) in Australia, [mySociety](mysociety.md) in the UK, and the [Parliamentary Monitoring Group](pmg.md) in South Africa. All of them attack the same accountability sink: a legislature that is technically public but practically unreadable, where the record exists but no citizen can realistically use it.

What distinguishes Mzalendo is the constitutional hook. Where equivalent organisations elsewhere argue that legislative openness *ought* to exist, Mzalendo can point to text that says Parliament *must* facilitate it — which turns a transparency campaign into an enforcement question.
