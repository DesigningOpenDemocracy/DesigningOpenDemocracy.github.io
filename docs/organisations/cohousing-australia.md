CoHousing Australia Cooperative Limited (CoHA) is a member-based, non-profit peak body advocating for cohousing — small-scale residential communities, typically 8 to 50 households, that are resident-planned and self-governed rather than run by an external landlord or developer.[^about] Its own network spans residents, academics, architects, planners, property professionals and general supporters of collaborative living, and CoHA is itself structured as a cooperative that anyone who supports the movement can join.[^about]

## Self-governance as the defining feature

CoHA's own description of the model puts collective governance ahead of the physical building form:

> "Typically, in Australia, cohousing communities are self-funded and self-governing."[^about]

This is the governance angle that brought CoHousing Australia onto DOD's radar: at [DOD's December 2022 Basil's Table event](../blog/posts/2023-01-21-podcast.md), architect and board-adjacent panellist Elena Pereyra framed her own cohousing work explicitly as "collective housing as a model for collective governance" — residents co-designing where and how they live, then collaboratively governing those shared spaces, rather than housing being designed and administered for them from outside.

## What CoHA does

CoHA describes its main activities as awareness-raising, support and advocacy for the cohousing movement — running a "Cohousing Connector" map and directory of forming and existing communities, working groups (including a planning-policy group and a collaborative-housing research network), and state chapters.[^getinvolved] It operates, in its own words, "without object of gain for its members or directors," with any surplus directed back into its objectives.[^board]
