BudgIT is a Nigerian civic-tech organisation founded in 2011 with a narrow, stubborn premise: a public budget nobody can read is not public. Its founding work was reformatting Nigeria's federal budget — a document technically available and practically impenetrable — into infographics, plain-language summaries and interactive dashboards.[^bg-found]

The organisation's mission statement makes the theory of change explicit: "To use creative technology to simplify public information, stimulating a community of active citizens and enabling their right to demand accountability, institutional reforms, efficient service delivery and an equitable society."[^bg-mission]

## From publishing to checking

The more interesting half of BudgIT's model is what came after the infographics. In 2014 it launched **Tracka**, which inverts the direction of information: instead of BudgIT explaining the budget to citizens, citizens report back on whether the borehole, clinic or classroom line-itemed in their constituency actually exists.[^bg-tracka] That closes a loop most budget-transparency work leaves open — publication of an allocation is not evidence of a delivery.

Its other recurring outputs include **State of States**, an annual assessment of the fiscal health of Nigeria's 36 states; a **States Fiscal Transparency League** ranking subnational governments on disclosure; and dashboards for federal budget analysis and primary healthcare accountability.

## Advocacy, and what it changed

BudgIT's **OpenNASS** campaign, begun in 2015 with EiE Nigeria, pushed the National Assembly to publish its own budget — a document the legislature had kept unpublished while scrutinising everyone else's. The campaign succeeded in 2018.[^bg-opennass] In 2017 the organisation also publicly challenged roughly N500bn of insertions into the federal budget.

In the same year it launched **[Civic Hive](civic-hive.md)** as its innovation centre — a Lagos civic-tech fellowship and incubator, tracked separately in this landscape, which describes itself as "an extension of BudgIT."[^bg-civichive]

## Where it works

BudgIT now runs country offices beyond Nigeria — in Ghana, Liberia and Sierra Leone, with a Washington DC presence at the Open Gov Hub.[^bg-expansion] The expansion is worth noting for what it implies about the model's portability: the specific pathology BudgIT was built to fix — large public budgets, weak subnational disclosure, and a wide gap between allocation and delivery — is not a Nigerian peculiarity.

## Why it belongs in the landscape

Budget transparency organisations are common; the ones that also build the feedback path from citizen back to institution are rarer. BudgIT sits alongside the fiscal-transparency and participatory-budgeting work this landscape already tracks, and its Tracka model is the clearest African example of a tool designed to convert published data into a specific, addressable demand on a named representative.
