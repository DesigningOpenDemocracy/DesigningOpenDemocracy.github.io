V-Dem (Varieties of Democracy) is an independent research institute headquartered at the Department of Political Science, University of Gothenburg, Sweden. It conceptualises and measures democracy through five high-level principles — electoral, liberal, participatory, deliberative, and egalitarian — collecting data across hundreds of indicators covering more than 200 polities from 1789 to the present.[^about]

## What it does

The V-Dem Institute is "the executive management arm of V-Dem with responsibility for most aspects of the data collection efforts, management and coordination of several large research programs, and for most of the outreach, dissemination, and collaborations with policy/practitioners’ organizations."[^about] Its flagship annual publication, the Democracy Report, is released every March and is free to download alongside the underlying dataset, scientific articles, and working papers.

## Democracy Report 2026

The 2026 edition, *Unraveling The Democratic Era?*, found that democratic backsliding is no longer confined to newer or fragile democracies. As the Institute's press release put it: "Democratic backsliding is now happening in well-established democracies. Democracy in the USA is deteriorating at unprecedented speed, and media and journalists are increasingly targeted across the world."[^dr2026] Six of the ten newly autocratising countries the report identified for 2025 were in Europe and North America, including Italy, the United Kingdom, and the USA.[^dr2026]

## Why it's on our radar

V-Dem's Democracy Report is one of the measurement projects most frequently cited by organisations across the Democracy Landscape when framing the state of global democracy — including [Folio Collective](folio-collective.md), whose September 2026 event on whether to "celebrate or reimagine" democracy opened by summarising the 2026 report's findings: "The Democracy Report 2026 by V-Dem Institute paints a gloomy global picture: democracy is ‘facing existential challenges around the world’."[^folio-event] V-Dem doesn't design or advocate for governance reform itself, but its data is the evidentiary backbone much of the rest of the landscape's advocacy and reform work relies on.
