The Australian Greens are a major Australian political party, formally established in 1992 through the federation of state-based Greens parties that had grown out of the environmental movement since the 1970s. Nationally, the party is best known for climate and environmental policy — but it also holds a specific, sourced record of governance-reform activity, distinct from that broader platform.

## Democracy-reform record

- **National Anti-Corruption Commission (NACC).** When the NACC Bill passed the Senate in November 2022, Greens-negotiated amendments gave the Commission's Inspector expanded real-time oversight and auditing powers over its own workings. ([Greens media release](https://greens.org.au/news/media-release/nacc-bill-passes-senate-historic-vote-critical-greens-amends-integrity))
- **Political donations reform.** The party's "Clean Up Politics" campaign has repeatedly pushed to cap political donations and ban donations from "dirty industries" (banking, mining, defence, gambling, fossil fuels among them), reintroducing legislation to that effect in February 2026. ([Clean Up Politics](https://greens.org.au/campaigns/clean-politics), [Feb 2026 media release](https://greens.org.au/news/media-release/greens-legislation-would-ban-dirty-donations-aec-data-reveals-major-parties))
- **Truth in political advertising.** The ACT Greens have pushed to extend South Australia's truth-in-political-advertising law — which lets the state electoral commission rule on and correct factually false campaign material — to the national Electoral Act. ([Greens ACT](https://greens.org.au/act/news/greens-push-truth-electoral-advertising))

## Position and caveats

The Greens are a general-purpose political party contesting elections across the full range of policy areas — climate, housing, industrial relations, foreign policy, and more — not a single-issue democracy-reform organisation. DOD is not tracking, endorsing, or making any judgment on that broader platform, and does not follow the party as a matter of course the way it follows organisations whose entire reason for existing is governance reform (compare [Pirate Party Australia](pirate-party-australia.md)).

The Greens are included here because the [accountability framework](../projects/accountability-framework/index.md)'s test is whether an organisation is working on *how* people participate in governance — mechanisms, structures, reforms, accountability systems — not whether DOD agrees with everything else it does. By that test, the party's anti-corruption, donations-transparency, and electoral-integrity record above is genuine, sourced, and sustained across more than a decade, not a one-off policy position. Tracking it here is no more an endorsement of the Greens' wider platform than tracking [Grattan Institute](grattan-institute.md)'s democracy program is an endorsement of its housing or energy policy work.
