The New Zealand Council for Civil Liberties (NZCCL) is an independent watchdog defending freedom of thought, expression, and action from government interference or restriction. Its work is primarily conducted through parliamentary submissions — a dense, consistent programme of legislative scrutiny that constitutes one of the most sustained civil-society oversight operations in New Zealand.

Recent submissions (2025–2026) span the full range of democratic-rights issues: opposition to a proposed move-on orders bill (arguing it limits seven Bill of Rights Act rights), opposition to policing amendments that expand warrantless surveillance powers, scrutiny of immigration enforcement powers, submissions on the Standing Orders review (restricting urgency, improving select committee processes, strengthening public participation), engagement with the Open Government Partnership's 5th National Action Plan, and a campaign to defend the right to protest (launching the Protect Protest Aotearoa coalition). The Council also runs an essay prize with Victoria University of Wellington Law School and hosts public webinars.

NZCCL is not a governance-design organisation — it does not build participation platforms or run citizens' assemblies. But it operates as the democratic immune system: submitting on every bill that touches civil liberties, maintaining institutional memory about rights encroachments, and providing the organised civil-society presence that makes parliamentary processes more than a formality.
