Digital Rights Watch (DRW) is an Australian charity, founded in 2016 at a meeting
of representatives from Australian digital and human rights organisations,
activists, political advisers, technology consultants and academics — called
largely in response to the introduction of Australia's mandatory
telecommunications data retention scheme. It describes its mission as ensuring
"Australians are equipped, empowered and enabled to uphold their digital
rights."[^drw-about]

DRW's work centres on transparency, accountability and oversight over how
governments and corporations collect and use personal data — privacy and
surveillance policy, encryption law, and, more recently, oversight of
AI systems deployed in government and healthcare settings. It works in
partnership with other Australian and international digital-rights groups,
including [Electronic Frontiers Australia](electronic-frontiers-australia.md),
Amnesty International Australia, Access Now, and Privacy International.

## Key work areas

- **Surveillance and data retention** — opposition to Australia's mandatory
  metadata retention scheme, the catalyst for the org's founding
- **Encryption policy** — led the "Alliance for a Safe and Secure Internet"
  coalition against the Assistance and Access Bill 2018, and made a formal
  submission opposing it
- **State of Digital Rights report** — an annual assessment of Australia's
  digital rights landscape, first published in 2018
- **AI oversight** — an "AI Harms Register" collecting reports of AI-related
  harms across government, business, healthcare and employment, and a current
  campaign on AI use in healthcare settings
