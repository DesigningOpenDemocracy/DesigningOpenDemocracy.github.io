Electronic Frontiers Australia (EFA) is Australia's longest-running digital
rights organisation — an independent, volunteer-run non-profit established on
1 January 1994, inspired by (but not formally affiliated with) the US Electronic
Frontier Foundation. It is incorporated under South Australian law and governed
by a volunteer ten-member board.

EFA's main objective is to protect and promote the civil liberties of internet
users, and it advocates for reform of laws and regulations that restrict free
speech, alongside public education on the civil-liberties issues raised by
computer-based communication systems. Its primary mode of work is parliamentary
submissions, regulatory complaints, and media commentary on privacy legislation,
online safety, encryption, data retention, and — increasingly — government use
of AI.

## Key work areas

- **Internet filtering** — a 19,000-signature Senate petition against mandatory
  ISP-level content filtering (2006)
- **Data retention and surveillance** — public opposition to Australia's 2014–15
  mandatory data retention laws and post-Snowden calls for intelligence-agency
  oversight
- **Robodebt accountability** — its then-chair co-founded the "Not My Debt"
  website documenting harm from the government's unlawful automated debt-recovery
  scheme
- **Facial recognition** — a co-lodged OAIC complaint that led to a formal
  finding that Bunnings' use of facial recognition technology breached the
  Privacy Act (2024)
- **AI in government decision-making** — public warnings, drawing on the
  Robodebt precedent, about the legal risk of AI-assisted government decisions
- **Freedom of Information** — a submission opposing the Freedom of Information
  Amendment Bill 2025, which was ultimately discharged from the Senate without
  passing (March 2026)
