Constitutional Clarion is a YouTube channel started in 2022 by Anne Twomey, Professor Emerita at Sydney Law School and one of Australia's most cited constitutional law scholars. It is a one-person public education effort rather than an organisation in the usual sense — included here as a "landscape of one," on the same footing DOD applies to institutions: what matters is the governance-literacy work being done, not the size of the entity doing it.

Twomey was appointed an Officer of the Order of Australia in 2021 for "distinguished service to the law, to legal education, and to public education on constitutional matters." Constitutional Clarion extends that public-education work directly to a general audience on video, alongside her long-standing role as a media commentator on Australian constitutional questions.

## What it covers

The channel explains the mechanics that most civic-education material skips past — the parts of the Constitution and its unwritten conventions that determine how power is actually exercised and constrained:

- The Governor-General's reserve powers and when (if ever) they can be used
- Constitutional conventions that aren't written down anywhere but govern how the system runs
- How referendums work procedurally, and why most fail
- Federalism — the division of power between the Commonwealth and the states
- Running commentary on live constitutional questions as they arise (referendum debates, court decisions, disputes over the use of executive power)

This sits squarely in DOD's interest in accountability *mechanisms* — a citizenry that understands how reserve powers, conventions, and referendum thresholds work is better placed to recognise when those mechanisms are being tested or bypassed.
