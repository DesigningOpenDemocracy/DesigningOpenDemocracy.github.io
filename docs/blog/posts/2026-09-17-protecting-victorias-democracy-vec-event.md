With Victoria heading into its largest-ever state election, Melbourne Press Club and the University of Melbourne are hosting a free lunchtime panel on what's being done to protect it from mis- and disinformation — with Victoria's own Electoral Commissioner on stage.

<!-- more -->

**Main lesson** —

- Election administrators are now treating mis- and disinformation as a frontline operational problem, not an afterthought — the VEC is publicly framing this poll as happening in "the most challenging communication environment ever seen."[^comms-env]
- Pairing an electoral commissioner with political journalists and a misinformation researcher on one stage is itself a signal: trust in the reporting and trust in the count are being treated as one problem, not two.

## What it is

["Protecting Victoria’s democracy ahead of state’s largest-ever election"](https://events.humanitix.com/protecting-victoria-s-democracy-ahead-of-state-s-largest-ever-election) runs **12:15–2pm on Wednesday 7 October 2026** at the [University of Melbourne’s Science Gallery Theatre](https://www.unimelb.edu.au/) (114 Grattan St, Parkville), and is free to attend.[^humanitix]

The framing, in the organisers' own words: "In a little over 50 days, the nation will be watching Victoria stage its largest and most complex event, the 2026 state election."[^scale] And it arrives amid what the organisers call "the most challenging communication environment ever seen" — "an abundance of electoral information from a myriad of sources is already being distributed across hundreds of platforms and channels, with dis-and-misinformation a real and present danger to a potential undermining of electoral integrity and trust in the voting process."[^comms-env]

## Who's speaking

- **Sven Bluemmel**, Victoria's Electoral Commissioner, "will outline the investments and initiatives the VEC is undertaking ahead of the state's election on Saturday 28 November."[^bluemmel]
- **Andrea Carson**, University of Melbourne Professor of Political Communication, "an expert in dis-and-misinformation."[^carson]
- **Sumeyya Ilanbey**, "Victorian political correspondent for The Australian Financial Review."[^ilanbey]
- Moderated by **Heidi Murphy**, Nine News' Chief Political Reporter.[^murphy]

Guests are invited to stay afterwards "for a democracy sausage and other refreshments."[^sausage]

## Why it's on our radar

DOD already tracks the [2026 Victorian state election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Victorian_state_election) — 28 November — on our own [calendar](../../calendar.md).[^vicelection] This panel sits squarely on our accountability-framework beat: a state's own electoral commission speaking publicly, alongside working journalists and an academic researcher, about how it's defending the vote from disinformation before polling day rather than after. Worth attending or watching for anyone following how Australian electoral administrators are adapting to a harder information environment.
