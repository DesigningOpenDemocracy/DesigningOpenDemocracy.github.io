<summary>About this calendar — what's listed here, and what the election cards mean</summary>
<p>DOD's own events (announced via the <a href="/blog/">blog</a>), plus organisations we track that have a public calendar feed (<code>ics_feed:</code>) or a manually confirmed upcoming event (<code>events:</code>).</p>
<p>Some organisations are in the Landscape because they <em>also</em> work on democracy: a customer-owned bank, a media outlet, a policy think tank with one democracy program among several. Most of what they run is about something else, so from those organisations only events an editor has marked as democracy-related are listed here. Their own pages still show everything.</p>
<p>Election dates are here too — federal, state and local polling days across Australia, plus a selection of major elections elsewhere. They're the one kind of entry with no organisation behind it, so they get a violet card and a 🗳️ badge, and there's an <a href="/calendar-elections.ics">elections-only feed</a> if that's all you want in your calendar. Where a date isn't settled yet the card says so: <strong>expected date</strong> (set by a fixed-term rule but not formally called), <strong>due by this date</strong> (the last day it can lawfully be held), or <strong>day not yet set</strong> (only the month is published). Listing an election is a statement about the date, not about the election.</p>
<p>This is a future-facing calendar of things to attend, not a historical record. The past year's major and notable events are in <a href="/news/">Landscape News</a>, and an organisation's own page has its full history.</p>
