title: Meta-Organisation
summary: "DOD's own description of itself: an organisation that pays attention across the democracy-reform landscape rather than running reform projects of its own. Distinct from the established organisational-theory term of the same name."
---

Some in DOD describe the group as a **meta-organisation** — on the premise that most democratic reform ideas being tried today have been tried before, somewhere, and the people building the next version are often unaware the lesson already exists. Rather than build a platform or run for office itself, DOD's role on this view is to pay attention across the whole reform landscape — tracking what's been tried, by whom, and what happened to it — so the lesson doesn't have to be re-learned from scratch each time.

It's a premise held by a few people in the group, not a settled institutional position. See [Philosophy](../philosophy/index.md) for it posed as an open question, and [Occupy to Plurality: what the 2010s civic-tech wave built, and where it stalled](../blog/posts/2026-08-07-civic-tech-wave-2010s.md) for it applied to a concrete case.

## Not the academic term

This is a different sense from the established use of "meta-organization" in organisational theory, coined by Göran Ahrne and Nils Brunsson (2005): there, a meta-organization is specifically *an organisation whose members are other organisations* — trade associations, the UN, ISO, and similar bodies — studied for the distinct dynamics that arise when members are organisations rather than individuals.[^wiki]

DOD's members are individuals, not organisations, and DOD doesn't govern or coordinate the organisations it tracks — it just observes and compares them, from the outside, with no formal relationship to any of them. The word is being reused here for its plain-English "organisation about organisations" sense, not the academic one. Worth keeping the two distinct if you're reading DOD's usage against the literature.
