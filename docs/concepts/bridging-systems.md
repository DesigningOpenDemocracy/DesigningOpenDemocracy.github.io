---
title: Bridging Systems
summary: "Systems designed to increase mutual understanding and trust across divides, including bridging-based ranking, which rewards content that appeals across groups rather than content that drives engagement."
---

**Bridging systems** is a term from Aviv Ovadya and Luke Thorburn's research on how to design systems that work against polarisation rather than amplifying it. The project's own definition: "Bridging systems increase mutual understanding and trust across divides, creating space for productive conflict, deliberation, or cooperation."[^bs-definition]

The idea covers both software and in-person practice. The authors "give examples of bridging systems across three domains: recommender systems on social media, software for conducting civic forums, and human-facilitated group deliberation."[^bs-domains] Their particular interest is **bridging-based ranking**: ordering what people see by whether it finds support across groups that usually disagree, instead of by engagement. In their words, they "focus particularly on the potential of bridging-based ranking to bring the benefits of offline bridging into spaces which are already governed by algorithms."[^bs-ranking]

It overlaps closely with [consensus mapping](consensus-mapping.md), which surfaces agreement in large-scale deliberation. The paper's acknowledgements thank Colin Megill for insights from his work on [Pol.is](../organisations/polis.md).[^bs-polis] Thorburn has also worked with Australia's [newDemocracy Foundation](../organisations/newdemocracy.md) on technology for convening deliberative mini-publics.[^bs-newdemocracy]
