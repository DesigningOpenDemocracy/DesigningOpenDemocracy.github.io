### Brian Khuu

- **Mission** (00:00:00). "Designing Open Democracy is an Australian [based]
  forum for engineers, social architects, as well as philosophers, who are
  aiming to explore the ways that democracy can be applied in the technology,
  engineering and design of social systems like governments and organizations.
  We are non-partisan and also agnostic to any particular democratic innovations
  that are out there, because… there are so many people with lots of different
  ideas… and we want to get their voice out to the public… And to also have a
  forum so that people of different innovation background and thoughts are able
  to freely express their ideas." The transcript's "operator forum" is almost
  certainly "Australian-based forum".
- **The five areas** (00:00:58–00:01:49).
  - Governance: "transparency, accountability… because whatever voting system you
    implement can be potentially hacked and subverted."
  - Structural: "a government system that accounts for the difference in
    philosophies and culture between, say, the city goers and the country
    people."
  - Economics: co-operatives, and "how companies don't have to be necessarily
    structured in… an authoritarian structure".
  - (Technology and sociological weren't elaborated in the recording.)
- **The 2020 programme** (00:03:33–00:17:39).
  1. Co-operatives / economic democracy: Antony McMullen, "who runs
     Co-operative Bonds", talk in March, workshop in April.
  2. "Building a general democracy and the role that technology can play":
     Adam Jacoby of MiVote, May/June, to learn "what has worked, what didn't work
     and what are the [impediments] in terms of getting acceptance in society".
  3. Citizens' juries: Nicholas Gruen, "coming back to us to elaborate… its future
     for over the next decade", and Kimbra White. Workshop in August.
  4. Participatory budgeting: workshop in October; "I'm not a very well-versed in
     the concept". Plans to contact "Bank the Table" (Bang the Table).
- **Podcast and directory** (00:22:29). "We actually been covering different
  organizations. One of the last one we covered is Flux. And the idea is that
  this also ties us in terms of our website of helping to have a list of all the
  democracy organizations that exist in Australia." This is the earliest spoken
  statement of what became the Democracy Landscape.

### Alexar Pendashteh

- **Running the poll** (00:01:49–00:03:33): "If you all can pick two… raise of

- **Format** (00:03:33): "For every area, we have one key speaker. And there's
  going to be a following month when we're running a workshop… one-to-many…

- **MiVote correction** (00:05:33): "MiVote wasn't a party. Flux is a party and

- **To Willow** (00:11:48): "a very good application of a process that already is
  practiced in the society for a different kind of context". Offers to send
  leads to CoE's workshops or co-run DOD's August one.
- **SOUP** (00:15:47): "you pay 10 bucks and at the end, there are four pitches of
  community projects and everyone gets a vote and all the money would go to the
  winner".
- **PB and tax** (00:16:43): "would this also be related to these conversations
  around the way we collect tax all the way to the top and redistributing it?"
  Kimbra: "Would be wonderful. You just got to get someone in political power to
  be interested." Alexar: that's a role for "technologies and data scientists…
  to demonstrate the impact".
- **The foundation** (00:18:30–00:21:38). The same diagnosis as the
  [Trust episode](2019-12-11_trust-concept-analysis.md), now with a proposal:
  - "There are different groups in Australia, that they're all doing amazing
    work… they all have their own ideas and opinions, not necessarily
    complementing perfectly well… But as a whole, this whole community is really
    disempowered because there's no direct source of income… we can't even call
    it an industry. So it's not financially empowered."
  - "A foundation, not an association… because an association… adds more work.
    Now, another democracy thing of democracy… Whereas a foundation is not
    attempting to bring everyone on the same page. In fact, it's the opposite. We
    want to empower everyone in what they're doing."
  - Uses: "an interface for this whole community so that it can financially
    interact with the rest of the world and receive funds and donations",
    contracts, research partnerships, links with overseas bodies and with
    governments.
  - "This is not our strong idea… if you see any flaws in anything I said,
    please bring it up because this is the best time for iterating on the idea."
- **2020 goals** (00:21:38): "more products of our events, like this podcast…
  commit to get a podcast out every month… not… an absolute hard target."
- **Structure** (00:22:29): "There's no company or organization even yet. It's
  just a group of people doing what they can… it's a flat structure."

### Kimbra White (MosaicLab)

- **Track record** (00:06:28). "We've been running citizen juries in Melbourne
  since 2014. Nicholas actually sat in on the first one we did for the 10-year
  financial plan for the city of Melbourne and wrote a lot about that one that we
  facilitated. We've probably now facilitated over 20 of them. We've got three
  running at the moment. So it's becoming very common in Melbourne."
- **Recruitment** (00:06:28–00:08:13). "You are randomly sampled of a database. And
  then once people have expressed interest, so you might hope that you get three
  or four hundred people… they are selected again to match the demographics…
  stratified random." Two methods: a physical invitation where "your address is
  checked", or open advertising, where "you need to get hundreds and hundreds of
  people… to make sure you get the randomness… rather than just the usual people
  who turn up all the time."
- **The database** (00:07:23–00:08:13). "The most typical one used is Australia
  Post." A council's rate base "is not good enough because you won't get the
  tenants", so invitations go "to the householder". "There are people who are
  specialised in doing… that sortition part of it."
- **Participatory budgeting** (00:12:55–00:15:47).
  - "I have never seen anyone do it at that scale or for a whole budget."
  - "Sometimes we don't use the word citizens, because there's an assumption that
    they have got the paperwork." A small but useful practice note.
  - "It came out of Brazil. It's been taken up quite widely in Europe,
    particularly in the UK… not a lot of take up of it in Australia." The
    classic form is face to face: "as a group… you look at all the different ways
    people want to spend a budget… and vote on that".
  - Online versions: Victoria's Pick My Project ("the biggest project I know");
    Melville Council's "Robin Hood Project… for the last five to six years".
  - "Most local councils have a community grants program… I haven't seen anyone
    seriously talk about budgets."
  - Definitions: "Some people would say that the City of Melbourne's 10-year
    financial plan deliberation was a participatory budgeting process… I would
    say it was a deliberation, not a PB process. But… what's a citizens assembly?
    What's a people's assembly?… people… out promoting deliberation would say
    that… it's got to be five to six full days of deliberation."
  - Bang the Table "is very well known amongst the community engagement world"
    and has a PB tool (00:17:39).
- She declines to suggest how DOD could work with MosaicLab: "I don't know enough.
  I'm sorry" (00:23:29).

## Checking the blog post against the transcript

1. **"see also the 'why can't democracy reformers unite' discussion from the
   following episode"**: the Trust episode (11 December 2019) came *before* this
   one. Should read "the previous episode".
2. **"Bank the Table"** in the post is the transcript's mishearing of **Bang the
   Table** (the Australian engagement-platform company, maker of EngagementHQ).
3. **"Adam Jacobi"** in the post: the MiVote founder is **Adam Jacoby** (as spelled
   in the 2021 MiVote post).
4. **The MiVote correction is reported without comment.** The post says MiVote
   "wasn't run as a registered party". DOD's own [2021 MiVote post](../../docs/blog/posts/2021-08-07-podcast.md)
   says "the Australian party deregistered around 2019". Check the AEC register
   and reconcile; see below.
5. **"Centre for Living Festival"**: the post's "Melbourne's Centre for Living
   Festival" should almost certainly be the **Sustainable Living Festival**,
   held in Melbourne each February.
6. **"SUP"** in the post is **SOUP** (Detroit SOUP; Melbourne had its own).

## Threads

1. **DOD's self-description has barely moved.** "Engineers, social architects…
   philosophers", "non-partisan and… agnostic", and "a list of all the democracy
   organizations… in Australia" are all still true of the site in 2026. Good
   primary sourcing for the About page and the DOD org page.
2. **The room wanted sociology, not tech.** Seven of seventeen votes went to the
   sociological vector against three for technology, from a group describing
   itself as engineers. Ben Ballingall's "the cultural shift isn't solved by
   people being able to vote", ten days earlier, is the same point.
3. **The fragmentation problem, second pass.** In December 2019 Alexar framed it as
   identity (no shared banner); here it's money (no income, no legal interface).
   The foundation is his answer. A future post could trace what happened to the
   idea.
4. **Definitions are contested even among practitioners** (Kimbra: jury vs
   assembly vs PB; "five to six full days"). Useful for concept pages that have to
   draw these lines.
5. **What happened to the programme.** COVID cancelled the physical meetups.
   The co-ops talk went online on 27 March ([Beyond CSR](2020-03-27_beyond-csr-economic-democracy.md));
   the MiVote catch-up came in July 2021; Gruen's return was recorded early, on
   3 March 2020 ([Isegoria](2020-03-03_isegoria-nicholas-gruen.md)); no
   participatory-budgeting episode was made.
