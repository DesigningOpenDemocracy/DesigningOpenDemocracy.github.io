## Austin, point by point

Quotes are verbatim from the **automated** transcript (fillers dropped, `…`
marks cuts). Check against the audio before quoting in DOD's own voice.

**On Hofkirchner's model** (00:00:52)

- "He had a sort of a two-tiered system… the open group, which anyone could join and
  vote in. And then a representative group, which is like a citizen jury selected…
  quite a graceful system." Leadership still matters, "but… maybe that doesn't have to
  be in the political system itself… There's a public forum debate, the media… and that
  filters through to these representative groups, which are literally statistically
  representative in a mathematical sense, not in a rhetorical ideological sense."
- On polling: "it's very, very rare that polls are wrong by more than 5%"; a UBI poll
  "came back with… 59% support and 20% oppose… that tells us something… When you
  actually ask people, they can surprise you" (00:01:52–00:02:53).

**Background** (00:03:43–00:10:48)

- The open-day lecturer: "Philosophy, the subject that I teach is useless… it's the place
  where we say we're not going to game our intellectual system to get a particular
  result."
- Beirut 2006 on exchange (Lebanese American University, living in Hamra); stayed on
  when the exchange agreement fell through; the war started "a few days after I would
  have flown home". His blog reached the Canberra Times front page, so journalism,
  "which was a terrible idea… in 2006, as people were just being fired". Freelance
  through an agency; "journalism is not generally a place for big ideas… It's for what's
  happening right now."
- Arab Spring: "the story of a lifetime… I had had a much more cynical… outlook for the
  Arab world. And some would say that that's been confirmed. But… I think people learned
  the wrong lessons from the Arab Spring."
- "My mom was a public servant, my dad's a left-wing bookseller… inner west of Sydney…
  the SBS left-wing demographic… I grew up sort of on [Noam] Chomsky… a Chomskyan
  counter propagandist… hyper aware that there's this big machine… spewing all this
  propaganda… But that can devolve into this tactical kind of nothingness."
- **Cairo, 2012** (00:09:01–00:10:48). After the Brotherhood won "one, two, three, four,
  five elections", the English-language press cast them as "the bad guys". Drinking with a
  "quite right-wing… Ron Paul kind of guy" from the financial press: "we were like
  absolutely agreeing… They should be allowed to finish their term. And if they don't…
  the military will take over and democracy is over… events were overtaking our
  ideological lens… what actually matters now is who knows their shit… who's been paying
  attention and who's been doing the work." After the coup, being "one little voice,
  shouting into this stadium of other people who were also shouting" wasn't enough.
- **Path to a product**: a transparent online magazine ("a data management nightmare"),
  then an NGO plus an open-source project ("we just didn't [attract] any engineers"),
  then "screw this. I'm just going to make software". Founded 2017; "only just now,
  nearly four years later, has released a product that we think is almost ready".

**The product** (00:12:42–00:22:28)

- Write In Stone Pty Ltd ("Stone PTY Ltd was taken"); open beta at writeinstone.com.
- Aim: "integrating with existing journalism workflows to… provide what I call
  epistemological struts… to help bolster the credibility of existing content."
- Demo: his fact-check of a Friendly Jordies video on vaccine supply (the 2021
  rollout at "7%"). A "research portal" under the article: "494 minutes… six events…
  59 highlights". At key moments he switches on his webcam to explain; the full
  screen-recorded research can be played back. "Not a technology focused company, we're
  a mission focused company."
- "When I say it's an audit trail… not just a degree of diligence… but your intent
  becomes more apparent… even if people get it wrong, if you see that they… were acting
  in good faith… you're going to be more forgiving… It's actually an emotional problem at
  its core."
- Hunchly (browser capture used by investigators and police): "the most similar product…
  I don't think people who are using [it] should stop… I think they should keep using
  [it] and show everybody else how… I want to be a force multiplier for all the existing
  tools."
- **Money**: research "is at the moment a net cost… someone… will invest 8 hours, 10
  hours, 40 hours, 400 hours in deep dive investigative work, and then they publish it,
  and 25 minutes later, thousands of people have copied it… research is valuable, make it
  visible." Plan: pre-roll ads on research video to pay journalists.

**Visibility as protection** (00:22:28–00:24:12)

- "Why are… construction workers on the road [wearing] high-vis? Because visibility is a
  kind of protection… if your evidence trail and your good faith efforts are visible, then
  that protects you from attacks… on your character… if your process of creating original
  work is visible, that protects you from people disputing your ownership."
- "Twitch has already got screen capture… This technology comes to journalism last
  normally… Sometimes you need secrecy. But when you don't need secrecy, you shouldn't
  default to it if your job is to… convince them of things. Not just tell them things, but
  show them the respect of convincing them and presenting the evidence."

**Journalism and trust** (00:24:12–00:31:02)

- Declines to characterise the industry; left Egypt for Ecuador in 2013 ("the world pages
  is 2% of coverage… really difficult to get stuff published from Latin America").
  "You've really got to think in generations."
- First load-bearing user: Scott Stedman of Forensic News ("evidence is key. Articles are
  always free. Clicks should not equal profit").
- "It's actually a good thing that… because the New York Times said so is no longer good
  enough… Or because Twitter said so… we shouldn't have this idealization of the old
  system." Chomsky's goalposts: the edges of acceptable debate define a consensus. "Now we've
  got a model where there are no goalposts and there is no consensus and everything's
  falling apart… that's actually very useful to bad actors… chaos is a ladder." The answer
  is not experts telling "Joe Schmo at home… you listen to what you're told… that's an
  intellectually bankrupt position. So the answer is more openness and more engagement."

**The model** (00:31:02–00:35:34)

- An information cone from the events and an attention cone from the public; "where
  attention and information meet, that's… public knowledge… And at the center of that is
  journalism." Social media moved the centre of gravity towards the public, "probably
  mostly a good thing". Fact-check filters sit between the story and the public; Write In
  Stone adds research content "closer to the actual subject matter, which then rebalances
  the system".
- The iceberg: "Most people will look at the tweet. Fewer… the story, and the smallest
  number will look at this, but that will have the highest quality of engagement… that
  will begin a conversation about what's an acceptably checked fact."
- "There's a canoe and that's the original news article… social media is like a sail…
  moves it faster… But also makes it more prone to capsize… what we are is the keel…
  adding a small amount of friction… creating… stability."

**Adoption and principle** (00:35:34–00:42:38)

- Forensic Architecture is "one of the people in the world I would most love to get using
  this tool… I'm in touch with them, and they're not that in touch with me back… it's
  about finding who's going to be that first person on the dance floor." "The early users
  you get, they become part of your company's DNA."
- "Mostly knowledge is produced in a civic public space… very rarely do you have to put on
  a trench coat and go down to a dark alley."
- **Strength through vulnerability** (00:38:11): "a fundamental enlightenment principle…
  Government should be electable, so they can be kicked out of office… falsifiability…
  when it survives, the meaning of that is really strong… bibliographies… making it easier
  to check if you're lying."
- Surveillance worries (Brian's body-cam and desk-sensor examples): "we have a pause
  button… it's not like an external surveillance system… we're concerned… that it might
  be used to overwork people… we're going to try and build in… digital ergonomic[s]."
- Metrics: time spent researching is the only value so far; "we've recently decided… When
  your mouth's moving, you're not researching"; ratings and "a matrix of values" to come.

**Close** (00:42:38)

- "We're actually the anti Twitter and we're not against Twitter… When you want to be…
  fast and flippant and fun, you go to Twitter. And when you want to take your time and get
  it right, you use Stone."
- "Other people want to build walls… and inside that fortress… legacy journalism can starve
  to death slowly… what we want to do is build windows, not walls… that's also going to let
  in some sunlight… this rift between the audience and journalists can be healed… so that
  the journalists can trust the public and the public can trust the journalist."

## Threads

1. **Trust in information as a precondition of democracy.** The most direct link in DOD's
   archive between journalism and democratic design. Pairs with the Trust episode (2019),
   Gruen on trolls and Pomerantsev (2020), and DOD's own
   [machine-verifiable citation](../../internal-heartbeat/machine-verifiable-citation.md)
   work: Write In Stone's "audit trail" and DOD's `quote:` verification are the same instinct,
   show the evidence, not just the conclusion.
2. **Strength through vulnerability** is a neat general principle for democratic design
   (elections, falsifiability, citations). Possibly a philosophy-page seed.
3. **Windows, not walls** vs gatekeeping. Austin rejects both the old consensus and expert
   filters; the Trust episode's "trust but verify" is the same stance.
4. **Hofkirchner's two tiers endorsed by a journalist.** Statistical representation for
   decisions, open debate for leadership, is a clean statement of the division Gruen and
   Hofkirchner both argue for.
