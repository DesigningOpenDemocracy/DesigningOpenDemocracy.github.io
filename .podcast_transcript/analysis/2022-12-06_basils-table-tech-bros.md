### Alexar Pendashteh

- **Potential** (00:11:18–00:13:57). "Electrical grids are the largest one-piece system that
  the mankind has made… it can just fail and you have to black out… I saw the very same
  principles… I can use the same to understand social systems." Hackathons: strangers form
  teams and present in a day and a half; "when things are facilitated and trust is

  Contracting across organisations, he saw where "workforce are organized in a way that they

  compromise as far as I've come to believe." (The same thesis as his 2019 Trust talk.)
- **"Is there an app for that?"** (00:45:19–00:48:48). Electrical engineering is "heavily
  regulated… certifications", but in IT, students "learn very quickly, and in the absence of

  "We can simply enable us humans kind of bounce information amongst each other, and tap into
  our collective intelligence. But we're not doing [it] because I think it doesn't translate
  into share[s] and assets… it's 100% feasible."

1. **Bridgette's quote is not in the transcript.** The post has *"I don't think I'll see a
   positive outcome in my lifetime, but I'm working on 150 years."* What she says is "helping
   people ask better questions might help shape a better world in 150 years… Not the immediate
   future", and earlier "I think it's about as good as it's gonna get for them right now". The
   quoted sentence is a composite and should be replaced with her words or turned into
   paraphrase.
2. **Jose's billionaire point is misframed.** The post says "This isn't a left critique of the
   right or vice versa — it's a structural observation." Jose says the opposite: "I'm a
   socialist… the truth to left and right is that there is a side that is squarely for equity…
   I don't think you can argue against that." His argument is that left-right is *real on
   equity* but breaks down elsewhere, and that both are products of Western modernity. The post
   should report his position as his.
3. **Twitter under Musk and Deliveroo's collapse** are presented as the event's framing, but
   neither is mentioned in the recording. They're accurate context for December 2022; label them
   as context, not as what was said.
4. **The three "design properties" bullets** (one member one vote, purpose-defined, resistant to
   acquisition) are editorial, not from the panel. Fine if framed as the post's own gloss.
5. **"At 70+"** for Basil isn't in the recording ("over 50 years" of social justice work is).
6. The Jose AI quote and Alexar's CI quote match the transcript.

## Threads

1. **Co-operation at the scale where people can practise it.** Elena's co-housing ("a good size
   for people to actually practice working together again"), Basil's elder ("we have to
   practice to become human"), and 888 itself. The same claim as the Beyond CSR panel's Sean
   and the IDoD panel's Anitra: democracy is learned in small bodies.
2. **The commons as a way past left-right** (Jose), set against Bridgette's "who's the we?"
   Jose's "implication" logic answers her: a safe climate implicates everyone. Whether that
   produces agreement, her 260 students suggest, is another matter.
3. **Who designs the systems.** Alexar (coders as unregulated solution-makers, when problem
   owners should hire them), Bridgette (co-design imposed on Parliament is still imposed),
   Elena (Airbnb extracting from a relational practice). All three are about decision rights,
   i.e. governance.
4. **"Isn't there an app for that?"** Antony asks the same question here and at the 2026 IDoD
   panel. The 2022 answers (Alexar: the wrong people are building; Jose: we *are* technology)
   and the 2026 answers (Godfrey: the app displaces organising; Nicole: automated deliberation
   is terrifying) would make a good comparison.
5. **Collective intelligence** is Alexar's constant from 2019 to 2022. It has a concept page.
