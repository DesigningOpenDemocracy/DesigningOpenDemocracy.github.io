### Rowan Dowland (Bank Australia)

**How he got here**

- "I was given a copy of this book on the very first day that I joined our credit
  union… I still carry my copy around… the paragraph that you read out tonight is
  one of the paragraphs that I share with all new people" (00:12:54).
- "If we lose sight of the values… the bravery, the sense of purpose that our
  founders had… then I think we actually lose something from our culture and our
  DNA" (00:13:46).
- Real estate first: "my values were not aligned to the values of the property
  industry, which were all about individuals, commission driven… It was about, I
  win when you lose" (00:14:39). Then about ten years at the Ministry of Housing
  and community housing, with homeless people and women's refuges.
- He wanted "a business model that combined the commercial discipline of running
  a business together with… a real strong sense of social purpose". His mother was
  a teachers' credit union "member rep"; "I'd never been a customer of a bank"
  (00:15:54).
- "There was a period of time with credit unions where they lost their way"
  (00:16:50).

**The bank**

- "The first cooperative bank in Australia in 2011… In 2015, we renamed that bank,
  Bank Australia" (00:16:50–00:17:45). Later: "our brand has only been in the
  market place really since 2017" (00:28:08). **The two dates conflict**; see the
  fact-check list.
- Germany: "about 45% of banking in Germany is done through cooperative banks".
- Scale: "When I started… we managed $50 million in assets. Today… around $7
  billion… And we would still be describing ourselves as sub-scale." About 72
  credit unions now form the group, including the ANSETT, David Syme and CSIRO
  staff credit unions (00:18:36).
- **The lesson of the late 1990s** (00:19:37). Callers in the Latrobe Valley:
  "You guys have just got bigger, but you've not got better. I don't understand
  why you exist any longer… you are no longer the staff credit [union], the pilots
  or [power] workers." At about $650m in assets "we were existing as opposed to
  having a strong sense of understanding why we existed".
- **The 2002 pivot** (00:20:28–00:22:12). After months visiting co-ops abroad: "we've
  defined the business… according to jobs or geography… I said the future is no
  longer jobs or geography or vocation or location. The future of cooperatives in
  banking… was attitudes and values. It's what people believe." It's "completely
  consistent with… cooperatives, banks that were formed in Germany in the mid
  1800s… groups of artisans and farmers who were unbanked".
- **Force for good** (00:23:03). "We need to be a good bank because you can't trade
  off values… But none of that's going to differentiate us… It's being a force for
  good that differentiates us." Customers' top issues: "climate change, renewable
  energy, fair treatment of refugees, closing the gap… mental health…
  homelessness, house affordability" (00:23:53).
- **January 2020** (00:24:45–00:27:17). "Almost 4,000 people joined us, switched out
  of the four major banks… because they wanted to empower themselves in a way that
  helped make a positive difference on climate change. It was the fires." Average
  age 34; "about 87% of them did not factor price into their decision". "People
  used to say to me banks can only compete on price… banks can compete on
  purpose."
- "Can a business exist for a purpose beyond profit maximization, I think we're
  about to give ourselves a tick" (00:28:08). Number 42 on a most-trusted-brands
  list.

**The Royal Commission** (00:29:05–00:33:27)

- "Not a single co-operative or mutual bank appeared before the Royal Commission.
  We all wrote submissions, but we didn't appear."
- Bank Australia sits on the Australian Banking Association as a member of the
  Global Alliance for Banking on Values: "We're the tiniest member of the ABA…
  like when I was a kid, the naughty boys used to sit on the card table at the end
  of the dining table."
- "It's because banks forgot why they existed in the first place… The four major
  banks… existed for the purpose of profit maximization and incentivizing
  executives… at the expense of their customer and their communities."
- "The penny has dropped with these guys"; which means Bank Australia must "raise
  the bar higher".

**Hope and technology** (00:46:33–00:48:21)

- "We were formed as [credit] unions… we were [a] movement. And a movement then
  became an industry. And I kind of want to take it back to being a movement, a
  movement for positive change." A daily social-media report shows "a conversation
  occurring somewhere in Australia now around the role of money in society".

**Markets that don't work** (00:48:21–00:53:47)

- "I fundamentally believe that security of tenure provides better quality
  outcomes… don't have to own it, you can lease it."
- **Indigenous home ownership** with Indigenous Business Australia: IBA and the
  government "carry some of the credit risk", so "unbankable Indigenous
  Australians" can buy. "You can actually help rebuild trust by way of
  partnership, and partnership comes naturally to cooperatives."
- **Community housing** (00:50:08–00:51:51). About 12 years earlier, when the Rudd
  government let community-housing organisations borrow, "no bank in the country
  would touch it". Bank Australia spent "probably two years… building the
  capability of boards and executive teams to understand how to take debt on",
  reaching about "$400 million worth of community housing investments". Then the
  government set up NHFIC, lending at AAA rates against Bank Australia's BBB+,
  and much of that lending was refinanced out: "it probably wouldn't have happened
  if the cooperative hadn't been there in the first [place]."
- "Cooperatives go into markets that don't work… how can you fix this?"
- **Clean Energy Finance Corporation** partnership: "the lowest cost home loan in
  Australia, bar none", for homes "one star beyond the statutory limit". When the
  model is proven "the four major banks will want to come in… Absolutely perfectly
  fine, because we seeded the market."

**Q&A**

- **Kids** (00:55:31–00:59:18). The movement "missed some amazing opportunities";
  CommBank's Dollarmites paid schools per account. Focus groups where people think
  the bank keeps their money "in a safe": "the level of financial literacy in this
  country is beyond belief". "If you don't want your money financing fossil fuels…
  live export… tobacco or gambling or weapons… don't bank with the [four] major
  banks." The long game is educating parents. The "face of Bank Australia"
  call-out closed "within 12 hours" at 160 stories, including a five-year-old
  saving "because I want to save the animals". Average customer age is falling;

- **Keeping purpose** (01:01:59–01:05:25). "Continually prove that the business model
  is successful." In 2002 the board asked for evidence: "I can't give you any, just
  trust me." Good governance, purpose in the constitution, measuring impact ("we
  will become a B Corp"), and succession ("Fiona Nixon is sitting there already").
  "Not a single conversation in the business of saying, we should do anything
  different."
- **Education** (01:06:34–01:10:02). After the 2012 International Year of
  Co-operatives, he, Melina Morrison and "Greg [Wall?]" set up the Business Council
  of Co-operatives and Mutuals. In 2012 "Co-operatives and Mutuals were not in the
  curriculum on any MBA anywhere in the country… I'm advised that they are now." A
  big-firm lawyer who advised clients never to form a co-op or mutual, not knowing
  Bank Australia is a company under the Corporations Act; the Act now defines
  mutuals.
- **Breaking the pyramid** (01:10:02–01:12:58). The BCCM; the "eight out of ten"
  awareness gap; ministers with responsibility for co-ops. But "they're not joining
  us because of our ownership model… they probably won't even know that… I can put
  you into a hypnotic trance [talking about ownership structures]. But… I would be
  able to engage you on a conversation around the role of money in society… a lot
  of us have used… language that just shut people down."
- **Word of mouth** (01:13:50–01:15:37). Each new customer tells "11 other people".
  Asked what incentive would help: "If you did that, I'd stop doing it. We want
  nothing from you… make us proud to be a customer of the bank."

## Threads

1. **Purpose vs structure.** Rowan's strongest claim is that customers join for
   purpose, not ownership, and that talking about ownership "puts you into a
   hypnotic trance". That sits uneasily with Antony's Trust-episode point that
   democracy only works when it's matched with ownership. A future post on
   economic democracy should put the two side by side.
2. **Losing your way at scale.** "Bigger, but you've not got better" is the Dunbar
   problem from the Trust episode, told from inside a bank. The fix was a new
   *community of interest* (values) to replace one of place and occupation.
3. **Co-ops as market-makers.** Seed a broken market, prove it, then let the big
   players in (community housing → NHFIC; green loans). A theory of change DOD
   hasn't documented elsewhere.
4. **Movement vs industry.** Rowan wants credit unions to "become a movement again";
   Basil wants circles of elders and the Power of Five. Both are about how
   co-operation is recruited, which Basil's final question presses and Rowan's
   answer doesn't settle.
