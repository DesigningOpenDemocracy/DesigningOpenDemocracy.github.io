## The event date is 21 October 2017, not 21 August

The blog post (filename, `date:`, heading, and the drafted summary's
"York Butter Factory, 21 August 2017") and the transcript filenames before
this pass all said 21 August 2017. Three independent sources say otherwise:

1. **Meetup's own page** for the event linked in the post body
   ([243645818](https://www.meetup.com/DesigningOpenDemocracy/events/243645818/),
   "\*Special Event\* - Citizens' Democracy; Presentations and Q&A", York
   Butter Factory) has `"dateTime":"2017-10-21T18:30:00+11:00"` (fetched
   2026-09-25).
2. **All three podcast intros** say "held on the 21st of October 2017".
3. **Hofkirchner reports the result of the Austrian election "last Sunday"**:
   G!LT at about 1%, the Greens falling from 12% to 3.8% and out of
   parliament. That was the 15 October 2017 Austrian legislative election, so
   the talk can't predate it.

## Per-speaker breakdown

Quotes are verbatim from the **automated** transcript (fillers dropped, `…`
marks cuts). Check against the audio before quoting in DOD's own voice.

### Nicholas Gruen

- **Vox pop democracy.** The 2008 Obama transition's online policy vote put
  legalising marijuana first, releasing the birth certificate second, and
  alien abductions third: "that's what came first, second and third in a very
  early attempt to bring the internet to bring us direct democracy" (00:01:50).
  The whole media system is built "to arouse your attention, your emotions,
  your disgust, your resentment" (00:02:43).
- **The carbon-price repeal.** "The greatest achievement of the parliament
  that assembled in 2013… was the blasting away of a difficult policy
  consensus that had been reached over the previous 15 years to price carbon"
  (00:04:39). Cost: "Ten billion dollars. A third of the budget deficit every
  year" (00:05:49). "80, 90 percent of parliamentarians thought it was a
  completely crazy thing to do." **His figures; see the fact-check list.**
- **Junk-food democracy.** "Our society is being optimized for the things that
  evolved on the African Savannah… food that is very close to being
  nutritious… is now poisonous. And that's roughly what's happening in
  democracy. We are getting pretty much exactly what we're asking for"
  (00:06:41–00:07:38). He uses the savannah line again in 2026 with the
  opposite valence: "we evolved on the African savannah to do that",
  deliberation.
- **Schumpeter's two ideas** (00:08:29). (1) Democracy has to "navigate the
  Cognitive Division of Labor", which is "the serpent in paradise" because it
  brings principal–agent problems. (2) Buying bread engages your rational
  faculties; in politics "there's no rational reason for us to vote at all…
  because we have an infinitesimal chance of influencing the result", so
  politics "is essentially run by emotion" (00:10:25).
- **Emotions.** "Feminine" emotions bind us together; "masculine" ones are
  "much more likely to come with some pretty nasty costs". "I'm not saying
  masculine bad, feminine good" (00:10:25–00:11:20). A framing a post would
  need to handle carefully.
- **Media.** Soundbites "collapsing steadily"; "politics as culture war. You
  know you're on a particular side, you know you're the good guys"
  (00:13:06). The "enemies of the people" front page: "a fine, Stalinist
  expression". The Daily Telegraph's switch on drug sentences: "Power without
  responsibility" (00:13:59).
- **Two ways to represent** (00:14:56). We need "cognitive elites. They don't
  have to be more powerful or more important than anyone else, but they need
  to spend the time trying to think about the issues. We can do this by
  election or… by lot." Elections as the touchstone of democracy only go back
  to the French and American revolutions (00:15:48).
- **Competitive vs unitary** (00:16:38). "You cannot beat a politician without
  beating other politicians… It's competitive and it is aristocratic,
  according to both Aristotle and Montesquieu." A jury: "your job isn't to
  beat anyone… your natural instinct is to compromise". Then the Greek word:
  "it doesn't mean freedom of speech… it means equality of speech"
  (00:17:29). This is the first appearance of isegoria in DOD's archive; the
  word itself dropped out of the transcript.
- **Hanson.** "Pauline Hanson, whether you like her or not, is an authentic
  politician, and people hear their voice much more through Pauline Hanson
  than they do through the brand management of many of the other parties"
  (00:18:28).
- **Evidence from juries.** The Adelaide nightlife jury; Melbourne's jury of
  "43 or however many… two or possibly three people stood for local council
  at the next election" (00:19:23).
- **His utopia** (00:20:22). "Where the 18th century and the 19th century was
  about setting up checks and balances between a lower house representing the
  people and an upper house representing property… we will now move towards
  checks and balances between the people as represented by their elected
  representatives and the people represented as they are in a jury."
- **Juries as activism** (00:21:15). "This would be a fantastically powerful
  form of political activism." A funded citizens' chamber in 2013 would have
  split "60, 70% pro-carbon pricing".
- **The magic trick** (00:22:10). "The politicians are the bad guys, we're
  always the good guys… When some dirty work is done, like keeping people out
  of our country, that's the nasty politicians… not the people who vote for
  the nasty politicians." "We, the people, need to take charge… That was the
  slogan for Brexit. That was the slogan for Trump" (00:23:59).
- **Tech fixes** (00:24:52). "It's all about the app, and the app's going
  to lower transactions costs… we need a few more transactions costs in there."
  Closing: "if anyone tells you this is a solution, get them also to define
  the problem."
- **Q&A: elected experts** (00:10:52). US elects judges; Athens elected only
  army commanders (*strategoi*), because "Athenian democracy was predicated on
  the idea that it was in a kind of duel to the death with oligarchy".
- **Q&A: corruption** (00:15:31). Bribing a juror would be criminal; one bad
  apple can be designed around, like Olympic judging. "The point about a
  Citizens Jury is that there aren't incentives to take more notice of a
  well-funded supplicant than anybody else." Selection integrity via
  scrutineers from "the Labour Party and the Liberal Party and the Greens and
  One Nation… and a retired judge" (00:18:19). "If we were in Ghana, maybe
  not" (00:19:13): a line a post should drop or handle with care.
- **Q&A: the debate** (00:21:00). Predictions about what *is* or *will be*
  aren't "direct observations of how good somebody's argument is about what
  should be the case". Prediction markets arrived with the same internet
  optimism as Joe Trippi's: "predicting the future is… not the same as
  indicating what one's values are" (00:24:35).
- **Q&A: the chamber's powers** (00:44:24). "I want a Citizens' Chamber to be
  part of our Parliament… in addition" to the Senate. Juries treat less than
  9 of 12 as hung, so modest majorities of 42 people mean little. His design:
  self-governing on 51–49 for procedure; perhaps a three-month delaying power
  (the Lords have one year); with 60–65%, it could **compel a secret ballot of
  the other chamber**, and use mechanisms like s.57's joint sitting
  (00:45:32–00:47:22). "The fact that you can end up in one means that you
  avoid a lot of them." Politicians would then "court the considered opinion
  of the people, not just the opinion of the people".
- **Q&A: politicians aren't the bogeyman** (00:47:22). "Politicians and their
  operatives are terrified of the people and they're trying to give them
  everything they want." Claims to sit on Gavin Newsom's gubernatorial
  campaign committee. **Unverified; don't repeat without checking.**
  "They're egotists and idealists… here they are in this toxic show. And
  they're trying their best" (00:49:07).
- **The line DOD quotes** (00:49:07), per the automated transcript: "in
  political combat, in our system, the considered opinion of the people
  amounts to nothing unless people respect it and pay it legitimacy." See
  "Checking the blog post" below: DOD has been quoting a different ending.
- **Slow democracy** (00:50:03). The NSW Drug Court example: try that "with
  the shock jocks… on the hustings". "The democracy we have actually does give
  us something pretty close to the will of the people and there's something
  toxic about it. So that's where I'm trying to build the legitimacy of…
  slow democracy." If people saw it, "they'd say, well look, those people,
  they're just like me, they thought about it a lot more than I have"
  (00:50:55).
- **Bad cop** (00:51:47). To Luca Belgiorno-Nettis: "you're the good cop, I'm
  the bad cop". newDemocracy asks governments "could we run a Citizens Jury for
  you to solve a little problem… good on them… But… doing that concedes the
  greater legitimacy for the system that we have" (00:52:38).
- **Oregon** (00:52:38–00:54:44). The Citizens' Initiative Review: 24
  jurors, a one-page statement for and against. On mandatory sentencing ("an
  absolute classic of Vox Pop democracy"), 21 signed against and 3 for, and
  the yes vote fell from about 70% to "56 or 7". "I'm after plonking this damn
  thing there and saying, look at that, that works better than you guys."

### Hubertus Hofkirchner

- **Why** (00:02:45–00:03:38). In the dot-com bubble, "while knowing that,
  I found the trap of believing this general madness and acting according to
  it". People "say what they think before they think".
- **Collective intelligence** (00:04:28). Freud thought crowds "become stupid,
  irrational, violent"; the Austrian School thought that "if you put them in
  proper processes, there's something evolving which we call collective
  intelligence. So the trick to make people think is to give them tools which
  don't produce madness." Twitter and Facebook "help with mobbing".
- **Iron law of oligarchy** (00:05:25). A Hayek Institute spin-off concluded
  "we should not have party democracy anymore". Michels (1911) → Hitler,
  Mussolini, Franco; next time "they're atom bombs at this position".
- **Prediki's method** (00:08:28). Participants "bet what it's gonna be… they
  don't just tick a box… which is when the brain starts to switch into a
  different type of thinking, a slower type… more deliberative". Then they give
  reasons (he calls it post-rationalisation), which are analysed for
  sentiment. In a normal poll Austrians expected things to improve after the
  election; once "made to think", they expected not.
- **The top grievance** (00:11:13): "people are most dissatisfied that
  everything will remain the same."
- **Referendums fail** (00:12:03). Brexit and "what is the EU?"; the 2015 Greek
  bailout referendum (62% no, then Tsipras accepted the package). A Greek
  interviewee "started to cry and said, I have no clue".
- **What motivates participation** (00:14:41): "that what people say, what
  they decide, actually matters… that these decisions are being implemented."
- **Parties refuse** (00:16:21). Three parties declined the "Bürgerparlament"
  (citizen parliament) model: "the leading people there, they all have cushy,
  well-paying jobs, and they were kind of fearing that they would put
  themselves out of work". Roland Düringer, "just about to found" his party,
  adopted it about three months before the election. It had been a protest
  list in the Beppe Grillo mould.
- **The model** (00:18:04–00:20:12). An open ideation phase where problems,
  solutions and objections are rated into an index; open surveys for
  diagnostics ("what is"); a prediction market for prognostics ("what will
  be"); citizen parliaments for decisions ("what shall be").
- **Expert identification** (00:21:23). People who predict better make better
  decisions (Tetlock's *Superforecasting*: foxes vs hedgehogs). "If you're
  negative over a series, then we know most likely you're not such a good
  decision maker." In the Q&A he says this screens out "people who are just
  nuts, psychos" before sortition (00:08:09).
- **Political bandwidth** (00:23:14). "Each of us is very good in one or two
  particular things… 1%, that's 80,000 people… we could have 400 parliaments
  in parallel."
- **The result** (00:25:02). First jury: the real issue was education, not
  immigration. About 1% on €120,000. Next time: a proper budget, activists who
  can present in national media, and "at least five Citizens Juries before".
  "Let's face it, nobody is interested in democracy, funnily enough, everybody
  assumes that it works… But what is about… taxes, their room would possibly
  be overflowing" (00:26:56).
- **Q&A: elect solutions, not people** (00:12:47–00:14:36). Like consumers
  choosing a phone: "they elect the solution, irrespective of the person…
  Any good politician has to be the type of man which every mother-in-law
  would be happy for her daughter to marry."
- **Q&A: capture** (00:19:13–00:21:00). Austria's fighter-jet purchase and
  money "sidetracked" to Burgenland; the Irish citizens' assembly on
  abortion, where "the Church could do nothing to impress the 100 citizens".
- **Q&A: "there is no such thing as *the* prediction market"** (00:24:35).
  Like search engines before Google: "somebody needs to develop a better
  mousetrap… Most of the stuff which you can find out is absolute crap."
  Prediki rates arguments plus and minus, not like/dislike.
- **Q&A: reflexive questions** (00:30:57). Ask people how often they speed and
  you get 5%; ask them to predict how many people will speed "and you will
  get much, much higher numbers. And of course we know which one is true."
- **Q&A: agenda setting** (00:32:50). The first jury ranked dissatisfaction by
  government department, then in one day chose the top issue by "systemic
  consensus" and went deep on it World-Café style. That output was the brief
  for the next round.
- **Q&A: binding the MP** (00:35:37–00:44:24). Parliament is already undermined:
  "it would be enough currently if there were five people sitting… holding up
  a little poster saying… I want with 43 votes". G!LT candidates sign a
  declaration, an oath before friends and family. His SA example: the nuclear
  fuel cycle jury said no by "70 percent or more", and he says Premier Jay
  Weatherill then floated a referendum before dropping it. Gruen: "I'm not
  sure that he did come out and call for a referendum… We agree about the
  moral. I'm not sure about the facts."

## Checking the blog post against the transcript

1. **The Gruen line DOD quotes may be misheard.** The post (and the
   [Taiwan post](../../docs/blog/posts/2026-05-25-taiwan-digital-democracy.md),
   twice) quote: *"In political combat, the considered opinion of the people
   amounts to nothing unless you consider it properly."* That wording comes
   from the human live notes (post line ~480). The automated transcript
   (part 3, 00:49:07) has: *"in political combat, in our system, the considered
   opinion of the people amounts to nothing unless people respect it and pay
   it legitimacy."* The transcript version fits the surrounding argument
   better (the next sentences are about legitimacy and press combat, and he
   goes on to call his project building "the legitimacy of… slow democracy").
   **Listen to the audio before either post quotes it again.** If the
   transcript is right, the Taiwan post's use of the line still works, and
   arguably works better.
2. **"Both speakers, independently, cite the post-Brexit spike in Britons
   googling 'what is the EU?'"**: only Hofkirchner does, in these cuts. Gruen
   uses Brexit (the £350m bus) but not the Google search.
3. **"GI!LT"** is a typo for **G!LT** (the list's full name was "Liste Roland
   Düringer – Meine Stimme G!LT").
4. **"York Butter Factory, 21 August 2017"**: see the date section above.
5. **"Ian Walker"** in the post and notes: newDemocracy's executive director
   spells it **Iain Walker**.
6. **"Since roughly 1989, only soundbites get through"** isn't in the podcast
   cuts, but is in the human notes (from his slides), so it's fine.
7. Human notes say "Geelong citizen juries example" at 00:58:27 of the full
   recording. In the transcript Hofkirchner describes a Melbourne financial-
   planning jury. See the fact-check list: he seems to have blended two
   newDemocracy projects.

## Threads

1. **Diagnosis agreed, cure contested.** Both say elections plus media produce
   emotion-driven politics. Gruen's cure is a sampled chamber whose authority
   comes from being *like us*. Hofkirchner's is an instrumented pipeline
   whose authority comes from *measured competence* (prediction scores) plus
   sortition. Gruen's jury is representative by construction; Hofkirchner's
   pre-selects "good decision makers" first, which is a real departure from
   sortition that the post doesn't name.
2. **Where authority comes from.** Hofkirchner: implement decisions and
   people will participate. Gruen: legitimacy has to be earned in public,
   because "the considered opinion of the people amounts to nothing unless
   people respect it". Iain Walker's point (via the host) is the practical
   version: juries are accepted when the press first shows jurors are "like
   them".
3. **Inside vs outside the system.** Hofkirchner runs a party and binds its MPs
   by oath. Gruen wants a chamber that "doesn't need anyone's permission" and
   calls newDemocracy's commissioned work the good cop. The 2026 IDoD panel
   replays this with MosaicLab (commissioned) and Gruen (standing assembly).
4. **Tech optimism.** Gruen mocks "it's all about the app"; Hofkirchner is
   building an app, but one designed to slow people down. A useful pairing
   for any post on democracy tech.
