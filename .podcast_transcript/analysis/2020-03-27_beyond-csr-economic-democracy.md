### Antony McMullen

- **Definition** (00:01:50–00:03:32). A co-op is autonomous ("shouldn't be controlled
  by any government or outside entity"), an association of persons (sometimes small
  businesses: "a group of stonemasons… their… owner operated business[es] were the
  members"), and "a jointly owned and democratically controlled enterprise". Social
  good; economic good ("democratizing the economy… sharing the rewards for effort
  through broadening our ownership"); cultural good.
- "Often when we think of democracy, we just think of the government, think of
  elections. But the cultural piece is actually making democracy work for you and I
  through these enterprises that we build and operate together" (00:03:32).
- **Two balancing values** (00:03:32–00:04:23): "self-responsibility. Yep, I'm going to
  do something about this situation… And solidarity."
- **Education** (00:05:18): in a worker co-op "you're not just turning up to work…
  you're knowing how to read financial statements".
- **Four hats** (Tim Mazzarol, UWA): patron, owner, investor, community member.
- **Types**: consumer, worker, secondary, tertiary, multi-stakeholder ("in Mondragon…
  the supermarket… is jointly governed by the workers… but also the people that shop
  there").
- **Platform co-ops** (00:07:55). Sharing-economy firms "make profit from people
  working with each other… but… don't actually include the people who are sharing
  their assets… the Uber drivers… are not actually owners". Stocksy: stock
  photographers who "decided to set up their own platform… the amount that comes back
  to the photographer is very, very different."
- **Profit-minimising** (00:10:34). "Co-ops make money to do good stuff. They're not
  doing stuff to make money… more accurately, they're profit minimizing. The surplus…
  is to cover costs… allow for risk, make provisions for the future and pay back
  investors." Unlike an association where "you might go to the AGM once in a while…
  Co-ops require something from members."
- **Ecosystems** (00:11:35–00:15:06). Evergreen in Cleveland (solar, Green City
  Growers, a hospital laundry). Earthworker "very much inspired by the Evergreen
  model", itself inspired by Mondragon. Earthworker Energy in the Latrobe Valley ("I've
  actually bought a solar hot water unit from Earthworker and it works really, really
  well"). Red Gum, from February 2018, 50+ clients, workers on two to four days a week
  aiming for permanent part-time. Earthworker is a non-distributing co-op whose members
  are distributing worker co-ops: "the co-operative commonwealth of these various
  worker co-op[s]".
- **Challenges** (00:15:06): free riders who don't patronise regularly; short- vs
  long-term conflict; "because there's not a share price… it's a bit difficult to
  find out how is this business going".
- **Starting one** (00:15:57–00:17:46): five members, rules, a formation statement
  for some types, registration (Consumer Affairs Victoria), the Co-operatives National
  Law ("everyone but Queensland… just about to come into"), a .coop address. Resources:
  getmutual.coop's rules builder, Employee Ownership Australia, the BCCM, a co-op
  power retailer, an organic-farmers' investment co-op, Co-operative Bonds.
- **Directors** (00:33:42). "Non-member directors always have to be in the minority…
  the non-member director does have a vote, just in the minority to protect and
  safeguard that member democracy."
- **Capital** (00:37:07–00:39:52). Co-op shares are fixed-value; you get your money
  back when you leave. Co-operative capital units let non-members invest for social
  and financial return without control, "but the problem we have is that no one's
  heard of a cooperative capital unit. Those that have heard of it are a bit scared of
  it… That nut's yet to be cracked."
- **Crisis** (00:42:24–00:46:19). "In this time of crisis, there will be plenty of
  needs… You're looking for lots and lots of small investments." Mondragon: "about
  70,000 employees… the seventh largest company type group in Spain… started directly
  after the Spanish Civil War… with education… Then they found a product… a particular
  kind of heater." In the GFC, when a big co-op failed, the ecosystem paid the workers
  "a living wage for a significant period… and… redeploy[ed] most of those worker
  owners." Advice: "have a look at what exists out there now… learn from them and then
  maybe be part of them." Also look overseas: "bakeries… organic grocery stores, window
  makers."
- **Second tier** (00:47:10–00:48:01). Earthworker and bHive are secondary,
  multi-stakeholder co-ops. A co-op of co-ops only "is technically what's called a
  cooperative group. And it only takes two cooperatives to form" one; it builds
  "infrastructure and support for the primary cooperatives".
- **Liability** (00:51:32). "All of the same limited liability protections… are
  available to cooperatives"; directors can still be liable; members may owe unpaid
  share amounts on a wind-up.
- **Decisions** (00:53:23). An elected, member-majority board governs; general
  meetings (possibly electronic) for big decisions, some needing "over 75%… So it is
  biased towards democracy, but it does also allow a board to get on and govern."
- **Pre-selling** (00:54:14–00:57:04). Member investment carries lighter regulatory
  burden than a company's, but still needs disclosure statements; external investment
  has higher thresholds. "It's a form of enterprise that marries up quite well to a
  crowdfunding type of ethos."
- **The tension** (01:06:10–01:07:59), answering Andrew. "There is certainly a
  diversity of people… described as socialists or… more conservative… the National
  Party in Australia is very, very supportive of cooperatives. We're also getting lots
  of interest from the union movement." The historic tension: co-ops as a fix for
  market failures, versus "a broader, more transformative movement. It used to be
  called the cooperative commonwealth… a lot of those people with those different
  perspectives still work together… I wouldn't be able to resolve that tension."

### Sean Bezard (Earthworker; Red Gum board)

- Background: four years helping run an autonomous community centre in Thornbury
  ("underage youth gigs… library, bike workshop"); a year coordinating the Melbourne
  University Food Co-op; about six years with Earthworker, drawn to it as "a response to
  the climate crisis which brought together economy and environment".
- **Red Gum's board in practice** (00:19:29–00:21:13). Five founding members, all
  cleaners, all on the board, plus a lawyer and Sean as non-member directors. "People
  took finance positions, secretary… a couple of people [were] delegates to the unions,
  and those positions have swapped over… so people get experience in different roles…
  We try as much as we can to have the members educated about the finance side." It
  grew from five to a peak of about 12.
- **Built backwards** (00:21:13): "as with a lot of things with Earthworker, we're a bit
  ass-backwards… it wasn't a group of co-operatives came together to build this kind of
  overlaying co-operative structure. We've kind of done it the other way around." The
  aim: "a just transition away from an extractive economy." He's done whatever was
  needed, "learning far too much about hot water systems."
- **Morwell** (00:22:05): a flagship "in a very economically distressed area… very
  dependent on coal fired power… to demonstrate that we can have dignified well-paid
  jobs".
- **The panel's gender balance** (00:22:05): "we noted that there was a bit of a boys
  club, two of the main founders of Red Gum, both women weren't able to be here
  tonight."
- **Influences** (00:22:59–00:24:45): Mierle Laderman Ukeles's *Maintenance Art
  Manifesto* and her residency with the New York Sanitation Department ("with the
  coronavirus… the most exploited and disposable workers turn out, actually, to be
  some of the most important"); Stephanie Kelton on "economies of care"; Thea
  Riofrancos's "decarbonize, democratize and decommodify".
- **Non-member directors** (00:31:58): "My position on the board is not to… contradict,
  it's just to bring the expertise and kind of go with whatever the membership thinks".
- **Advice** (00:41:34–00:42:24): "Move fast and break things… if you're thinking like a
  cooperative… is move slow and repair things… in a systematic way… think long term…
  what's your niche?… You've got to have a business case for it because we're
  operating in a capitalist system… And lastly, it's all about the people."

### Ian McBurney (bHive)

- Twenty years in environmental education and culture change; four years on bHive,
  "thinking about how we could bring together the ecological and the social and the
  economic" (00:25:51).
- **Villages** (00:25:51–00:27:34): "local people, local place, and local economy…
  we own our digital sharing economy together". Members join a village of people within
  two kilometres to share goods, skills and events. Non-distributing: surplus goes "back
  into the local community and into improving the platform. When you become a member…
  you own Beehive, you own your data… no one will advertise at you." "We've always
  talked about getting an epidemic of belonging… at the moment we've got an epidemic
  that's creating social isolation." Launch planned for the end of August 2020.
- **The city layer** (00:27:34–00:30:14): a car-sharing co-op with Bendigo businesses as
  the first distributing member; later energy, water, food and monetary co-ops.
  "Two and a half billion dollars spent by Bendigo households every year… most of that
  leaves Bendigo and most of that leaves the country." "We want to grow the Beehive
  Co-operative to the size of the Bendigo economy, and then it doesn't need to grow
  anymore… instead of scaling like a startup and taking over the world… each local
  place to own their own local economy."
- **Advice** (00:34:32–00:37:07): "Get together with five other fired up people";
  there's a much stronger support network than five years ago; the global platform-co-op
  movement helped; "talk to people that have done a lot of hard work and see if you can
  replicate rather than trying to start from scratch." "Capital raising has been
  really, really, really hard. We have done five years of hard yards there and we still
  don't have an answer… we're still volunteer board members." People won't invest "in
  something they can't see and touch and hold".
- **Identity and reputation** (00:48:56–00:50:38): "every member will have their own
  identity and their own reputation. That starts in their geographic village… and that
  will network through all the different cooperatives… open source ideas… if one
  cooperative in one place has an idea, they just share it."
- **The pandemic** (00:57:04–00:59:49): "casual workers… are all two or three weeks away
  from poverty. And directors of large corporations are three months away from
  bankruptcy… the global economy is a house of cards. And we don't have a cooperative
  network in Australia… strong enough." "40 emails and phone calls this week… 'be hive
  ready'… It's not quite big enough in Australia to make the impact that it will make in
  the future."

### Andrew (Downing), audience

- **Co-operation around capitalism** (00:59:49–01:05:19). "I'm very much a systems
  thinker… I get a very sort of… anti-capitalist vibe to the whole movement… but then I
  hear people having trouble raising capital. Contradiction right there… there's a focus
  on this being cooperatives versus capitalism instead of… a cooperative, augmented
  capitalism… every one of those [breaks] is an opportunity." Coles and Woolworths
  "crush the farmers… Look, there's an opportunity." The most stable co-ops are "existing
  businesses, particularly small businesses that can find a way to cooperate with each

  they put very little emotional energy in and they get some positive gain out of it…
  a nice smooth curve."
- "Anybody who's on the outer… being squeezed out by monopolistic behaviors is usually
  somebody who has something to lose that could actually benefit by cooperation."

## Threads

1. **Democracy as economic structure.** Antony's "the cultural piece" and Sean's
   rotating board roles are the most concrete description in DOD's archive of what
   participation *does* to participants, alongside Gruen's jurors. A post could pair
   them: juries and co-ops as two schools of democracy.
2. **Scale without extraction.** Ian's "grow to the size of the Bendigo economy, and then
   it doesn't need to grow anymore", Earthworker's commonwealth, Mondragon's ecosystem.
   Rowan Dowland's "bigger, but not better" from the [banking Basil's Table](2020-02-25_basils-table-banking.md)
   is the warning.
3. **Capital is the bottleneck** for everyone on the panel. Co-operative capital units
   that "no one's heard of", five years of fundraising, investors who want control.
   Andrew's challenge (co-operate around capitalism's breaks) is partly a response to
   this, and Antony's answer names a real, unresolved tension in the movement.
4. **Trust and onboarding.** Andrew's "commensurate returns" is the Trust episode's
   loss-aversion point turned into design advice.
5. **Identity and reputation as co-op infrastructure** (Ian, Alexar): where this
   episode meets DOD's tech interests. Worth following up what bHive actually built.
