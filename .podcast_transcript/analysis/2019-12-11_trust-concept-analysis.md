### Alexar Pendashteh

- **Why he cares** (00:02:43). Each scientific layer emerges from the one below
  "when they are in a certain [flow]". "What interests me is what kind of things
  can actually emerge from this layer? What are the things that we collectively
  can achieve… that currently at the moment we're not? Because we're not yet in
  enough harmony" (00:03:36). He wants "the mechanics and physics of collective
  potential".
- **Trust as perception** (00:05:22). "The whole trust is a perception game, and
  that's what makes it tricky, because you have to perceive the perception of
  the other person." Later: "I see trust as the perception of the other person's
  perception… that's why trust is a bidirectional phenomenon" (00:12:52).
- **Where it comes from** (00:05:22–00:07:08). "The key factor that has helped us
  get to this point is the fact that we could cooperate in a larger scale…
  because… we could form an alliance." Language lets two people "understand we
  both share a certain value". "We will protect the tribe rather than ourselves."
- **Resonance** (00:08:01). "When a group of waves can create a tide, if they are
  in sync… we have built institutes or mechanisms, layer on layer on layer, on
  this core, which is the alliance, which is the trust."
- **Organisations** (00:09:45–00:12:52). At scale "we have to substitute trust with
  some other mechanisms"; in a company "it is the law" that holds people
  together. Those forces "are artificial and arbitrary"; if relationships were
  "based on trust… the result would be much, much different and more harmonic".
  Startup teams "work much harder with much less certainty" than a managed team
  could ever get "even 10 percent of".
- **Work designed for distrust** (00:15:36–00:17:25). "The whole work structure
  is designed based on the assumption that when I go to work, I resent it…
  every process is put in place to make sure I deliver enough for the business
  before I run away. I'm exaggerating a lot here, but you can trace this in all
  terminologies, the language, the rules." We keep structures built for work we
  no longer do. And distrust breeds distrust: processes that treat you as
  untrustworthy make you "want basically to contribute less" (00:18:21).
- **Identity anchors** (00:33:52). "To form alliance… you need to be able to
  identify as a group. You need anchors, you need words… in the past, traditions
  or religions would serve that purpose… as we're losing that, we are
  subconsciously… trying to find those identities that we can grasp on."
- **Impeachment as trust** (00:35:45–00:37:38). Republicans stay united
  "regardless of the argument, and regardless of how bad the evidence is"; they
  "all need to trust the fact that none of them is gonna kind of split". "I'm not
  suggesting it's good or bad." Perception can flip suddenly, as with a false
  rumour about a Greek loan that sank the euro; "the recovery is not that easy"
  (00:38:30).
- **The reformers' problem** (00:45:17–00:48:09). Effective groups are tribal and
  make compromises; principled people won't, so "there's no big group that is
  effective". Democracy groups "are collectively… in a minority… So logically,
  they all need to be together, have one voice, to push this big stone… But what
  actually we see happens is that they get into internal arguments, because…
  there is no one big word that everyone can strongly identify. Whereas those
  with much less… honorable principles… choose to make compromises, and they
  identify under one banner, that gives them power." Brian's audio note names
  the groups: SecureVote, Horizon State, NewVote, Flux, MiVote, newDemocracy,
  DOD. The challenge: "without needing to identify as one thing… find one thing
  that brings all of us… together, and act as one entity and still be effective…
  without needing to compromise on any of your values."
- **Method** (00:49:01). "When you actually analyze how a concept like trust
  comes to be… you can actually find those triggers in the system… or set up
  the environment for the solution to emerge." Electromagnetism → Wi-Fi.
- **Farmer co-ops and a ledger** (00:49:56, 00:57:02). Research on why co-ops
  fail; farmers are easy to exploit through cash-flow desperation and
  separation. A Brisbane startup gave farmers phones running a ledger: "they
  don't need to trust any of their own management… I need to trust the system."
  In coffee in Kenya, "the farmer's income was increased to three times more",
  and they then had to help farmers manage the surplus. "In theory the system
  was correct, but the human element was kind of breaking the system. And an
  intervention of technology allowed for humans to reestablish trust" (00:58:49).
- **Superorganisms** (00:58:49). With a reliable base layer ("could be this
  blockchain thing, could be Orthodox Judaism"), you "can lose self in a way,
  like a cell in a body… like the colony of ants".
- **Blockchain as governance** (01:14:45–01:17:43). Bitcoin was released
  anonymously and "you don't need any authority to operate it… without needing
  anyone to trust anybody, collectively, you can have consensus on how much
  everyone has." "All IT was about what we do with information. After
  blockchain, people have started thinking about who does thing with
  information, which was about more governance." It brought technologists and
  "philosophers, economists, and social change makers" together.
- **Libra** (01:17:43). IT let rival corporates "trust each other, to form
  alliance… to take over something that… traditionally belonged to… the
  government, issuance of money".
- **The feedback loop** (01:22:19–01:24:08). Network effects make platforms hard
  to leave. "Who is influenced is not who is influencing it… the team behind
  Uber, Facebook… are influencing the platform by decisions they make. But an
  entirely different group of people are influenced by the platform… And this
  disconnect is the root cause of issues, which is something like cooperatives
  would solve. A concept of commons… would also solve that. When you close this
  loop, if someone is influencing something, they also need to be influenced the
  same degree… In engineering, that's called feedback loop." Someone else
  supplies "destabilise"; he ends: "cooperative… it's a vehicle to kind of close
  that loop."

### Andrew Kay

- **Dunbar's number** (00:08:01–00:09:45). "About 150… Beyond that, you need

  tribe, and we agreed on the rules. And yeah, the bigger it gets, the more
  rules you get."
- **Collectivising** (00:17:25). "If I act alone, I have to just trust everyone
  acts in my interest. But if I get everyone to work as a collective, then we
  have more power and I don't have to trust as much." When trust breaks: "do you
  still continue to cooperate… or do you go for payback?"
- ***Survivor*** (00:13:53) as the popular version of the prisoner's dilemma.
- **The Southern Strategy** (00:25:40–00:27:26), told from memory, including

- **Leigh Sales and a flipped worldview** (00:34:43). He relishes "cognitive
  dissonance… when people… flipped upside down in an instant". Then: millions of
  Australians had "their awakening moment" about Aboriginal people.
- **"Sides are stupid"** (00:32:59). "Calling yourself left or right is so
  ludicrous, because there are a million issues."
- **Penny Wong** (00:39:21–00:41:52). His speculation that Gillard, in minority
  government, asked Wong to hold the party line against marriage equality: "pure
  tribalism… I am going with the tribe over my own short-term needs." **This is
  his guess about a private conversation. Don't repeat it as fact.**
- **Tribalism's two edges** (00:41:52). "In almost any system, the good points and
  the bad points are the same thing… A tribe is loyal to each other. That is
  good. A tribe can end up being loyal… in a way that flies in the face of logic
  or decency, that is bad."
- **Tribal epistemology** (00:41:52–00:42:42). Looked up on the night: "when the
  tribal bond rejects actual epistemological principles. In fact, you stop trying
  to find an objective truth because it could weaken the tribe… It's not limited
  to the right… It's not limited to the US, but you really see it… in the extreme
  right, in the US… an aggressive rejection of facts." The term is David Roberts's
  (Vox, 2017); the source wasn't named on the night.
- **Putin** (00:44:27). "If you're going to smash my country, I'll smash yours",
  via fake Facebook pages on both extremes: "if I start a fight, it doesn't matter
  who wins. The fight weakens the people who are fighting."
- **Agricultural price support** (00:51:44–00:53:28): milk, the wool stockpile,
  and "it was the conservatives who did it". See the fact-check list.
- **Trust but verify** (01:10:15, 01:11:59). "Any system that asks me to trust
  something, better have some very convincing testing functions… if you want to
  give me nightmares, start talking about digital voting, electronic voting
  machines, or worse still, internet-based voting… That's the worst thing I can
  think of." Looks up the Russian proverb and Reagan. "If you can't verify your
  trust, you're in a religion if you're just trusting blindly."
- **Uber** (01:19:30–01:22:19). Governments legalise popular platforms rather
  than fight them: "That's real political cowardice."

### Antony McMullen

- **The ownership spectrum** (00:19:17–00:21:58). Broad-based employee share
  ownership at one end, worker co-ops at the other. Co-ops raise the Dunbar
  problem: "you can't have everyone in the room making decisions for absolutely
  everything… So how do you actually structure teams… to have responsibility
  and then being democratically accountable?" "If you don't match the democracy
  with the ownership, like that to me presents some problems. So that's where
  economic democracy… comes into it." "We sometimes… think of [democracy] as
  something apart from things like ownership rights, whereas… we need to think
  about those two things in tandem."
- **Co-op rules** (00:23:42). No member may hold more than 20% of issued share
  capital; "no matter what, it's one member, one vote". Extra shares reward
  investment, not votes. External investment "is a real challenge for co-ops.
  Because you wanna keep the external investors away from the decision-making".
- **Dairy** (00:53:28). Wesfarmers began as a co-op; Murray Goulburn "took on a
  lot of external investment that wasn't members, and they wanted to return on
  investment… They took some risks internationally."
- **Co-ops and the prisoner's dilemma** (00:54:22). "You'll have winners and losers
  living in that group, and you've got to feel comfortable with losing short
  term, having a long term gain for everyone. That doesn't really sit well with
  the rest of our economy." Monkeys "hate losing more than they like winning".
- **Money** (01:02:40–01:03:45). Markets are fine where ownership and participation
  mean something, but not once investments are "packaged up… and sold"; "the
  money is to do stuff, rather than doing stuff to make the money".
- **Platforms** (01:19:30). "It's important who owns these platforms. So if the
  drivers themselves are owning the platform… then I'm kind of OK with it."
  Uber Eats takes "something like… 30%" from restaurants; Airbnb and Tasmanian
  housing.

## Checking the blog post against the transcript

1. **The feedback-loop quote is a composite.** The post quotes it as one
   continuous line. It's assembled from several sentences in 01:22:19–01:23:13,
   and "destabilises" comes from another speaker. Fine as a paraphrase; if it
   stays in quote marks, it should use `…` for the gaps.
2. **"Bigger tribe, more rules. The bigger it gets, the more rules you get."**
   The transcript has "It's a bigger tribe, and we agreed on the rules. And yeah,
   the bigger it gets, the more rules you get." The post's version is tidied.
3. **"This was the first episode of the DOD podcast"**: the intro says "the first
   of a two-part series on Trust" and "as this is a new podcast". The 2017
   recordings were released later as retrospectives, so "first episode recorded
   for the podcast" is right; "first episode" could mislead.
4. **Chomsky's "dictatorship bubbles"** is Alexar's phrase as transcribed.
   Chomsky's own term for corporations is "private tyrannies". Worth a note if
   the post keeps the attribution.
5. The post's reading of the reform-groups list (SecureVote, Horizon State,
   NewVote, Flux, MiVote, newDemocracy, DOD) matches Brian's audio note.

## Threads

1. **Trust doesn't scale; rules replace it; rules breed distrust.** Dunbar →
   law → workplaces designed for shirkers → people contribute less. This is the
   episode's spine, and it's the same arc as the Flux and MiVote episodes'
   "politicians can't be trusted, so bind them by design".
2. **Co-operatives as the fix, and why they fail anyway.** Antony's ownership-
   democracy link, Alexar's farmer co-ops, Murray Goulburn. The answer offered
   is technology as a trust layer, which Andrew immediately qualifies ("trust but
   verify"). DOD returns to co-ops in the three 888 episodes.
3. **Tribalism is how groups become effective, and how they stop reasoning.**
   Alexar: "It's all about power". Andrew: tribal epistemology. The reform-
   groups problem is the same trade-off: principled groups don't compromise,
   so they don't cohere.
4. **Blockchain as governance technology** (Alexar) vs **internet voting as a
   nightmare** (Andrew). Both positions recur: Horizon State's blockchain voting
   story in the 2021 MiVote episode, and DOD's verifiable-voting concept page.
5. **The feedback loop** is DOD's accountability-sink idea stated in 2019.
