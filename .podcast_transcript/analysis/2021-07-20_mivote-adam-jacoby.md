## Adam Jacoby, point by point

Quotes are verbatim from the **automated** transcript (fillers dropped, `…`
marks cuts). Check against the audio before quoting in DOD's own voice. He
swears freely on the recording; quotes below keep it where it's the point.

**What MiVote is** (00:00:00)

- "A new model of democracy effectively. It started from an examination of the
  philosophical ideas of what democracy was meant to deliver. It then worked through
  the erosion points… and then looked at the interventions that could be made to
  create a purer version of democracy… the equality of the democracy is not
  determined by an election, but rather… by the way citizens outline and then decide
  what policy decisions should be made."
- Brian: one of DOD's first gatherings was at MiVote's office (00:01:18).

**Origins** (00:02:13–00:07:29)

- "A democracy activist… on four continents… at Davos, at Stanford, March for Our
  Lives." Singularity University's first Global Grand Challenge Awards: finalists,
  "inundated by nations who wanted to see if they could use our technology and start
  a chapter". "In hindsight [we] grew faster than we should have and tried to do more
  than we should have with the resource that we had."
- Left his last startup to think about democracy; interviewed "religious leaders and
  political leaders and business leaders and average everyday citizens" to see if the
  "erosion points" were global.
- Met co-founder Hamish Hughes at a Cambridge alumni event where "everybody there was
  about 75 years old, except for one other guy".
- Free morning coffee at a South Melbourne café: "the first one… five or six people…
  A couple of weeks later… 20… a couple of weeks after that… 50." Volunteers built it.
  Names: Frame Democracy → OB4 ("of the people, by the people, for the people") →
  MiVote, registered as a not-for-profit.

**The model** (00:07:29–00:09:13)

- "You can't enact the people's will if you don't ask the people what they want…
  when it comes to climate change, or… tax reform, or… domestic violence, or… putting a
  fucking tram stop in, what do you want?"
- Then the obstacles: "people can buy their way into those outcomes… the influence of
  money in politics… the tenure of politicians… how do you make sure people have an
  informed voice and an informed vote, rather than… voting on the color that they
  normally vote for".
- Structure: "wasn't particularly hierarchical… The mistakes that we made were we were
  probably too open… everybody had an opinion and everybody wanted to be heard all the
  time." An organisational-democracy lesson from a democracy organisation.

**The votes** (00:09:13–00:11:59)

- "Jamie [Skella] worked on building and architecting the first blockchain voting
  platform in the world, which MiVote created." Six trial votes; "by the last vote, we
  were having four or five thousand people voting".
- "Four different choices… not aligned to political party positions… a destinational
  approach… which path would you like your government to take? We provided information
  packs for each of the four choices." Approval-style: "you can choose as many or as few
  as you think are in the best interests of your country."
- "Our constitution requires… that no vote can become policy unless there's 60% voter
  approval… everybody said it wasn't possible… We never, ever had less than 60%."
  **Worth a methodological note in any post**: under approval voting, a voter can
  approve several options, so 60% approval for one direction is a different (lower)
  bar than 60% of voters choosing it alone. And the voters were self-selected.

**Horizon State** (00:11:59–00:13:48)

- The board didn't want the movement to become a tech company, so it spun one out and
  "handpicked the three people, one of which was Jamie Skella… We didn't own any of it,
  not 1%. We didn't sit on the board." The agreement: build for MiVote first and donate a
  percentage of revenue back.
- "It was the single worst commercial decision I've ever made in my life… they didn't
  do any of the things that they promised us. We had very little recourse… cost MiVote
  probably five years and hundreds of thousands of dollars." **This is one side of a
  commercial dispute. Attribute it, and don't characterise Horizon State's conduct in
  DOD's own voice.**
- Now: "MiVote technologies is for-profit. MiVote, the movement, is not-for-profit…
  part of the same umbrella… housed in the United Kingdom."

**UK and India** (00:13:48–00:15:30)

- "The first not-for-profit ever to be invited into… the PEG program… out of the
  Department of Prime Minister and Cabinet… the Entrepreneurs Program… incentivise
  technologies from other countries to move their headquarters to London." See the
  fact-check list.
- "Joydeep Mondal… used to run our Indian office, is now the CEO of MiVote globally."

**Trump and the trials** (00:15:30–00:18:18)

- "I don't think it's the pandemic… Trump's reign was probably the tipping point… I kept
  saying, we're edging closer and closer to something that looks like fascism, because
  people have forgotten what democracy actually means. And we're measuring the quality of
  our democracy by the wrong metrics. And everybody thought I was… crazy… Trump was the
  biggest gift to MiVote ever."
- "The contract is signed with a member of Federal Parliament in Australia who will be
  testing and running a series of votes… in his electorate, over the next six months."
  Also "the EU… union movements across Europe… candidates in India". "Those trials will
  lead us to an IEO, where we're going to offer the product to the market, probably in
  about six months' time." "It's been nearly a decade."
- Lessons: "walking before you can run, trialling the product… an integrated approach
  to the relationship between the technology and the charity… having people that you
  can trust who run parts of the business."

**Other models** (00:18:18–00:22:40)

- "It's no secret that I'm not a big fan of flux."
- Taiwan "is probably the best example of citizen-based policy making in the world…
  the government effectively opens the doors and says… for the next month, we're going
  to focus on a policy related to X… anyone is welcome… they've already enacted about
  150 pieces of legislation… crafted by the citizens. The problem is that it is only the
  citizens who show up… you're not finding a way to engage the citizenry in any
  meaningful way in terms of volume."
- "Most citizens don't understand legislation, nor should they have to." Binary votes
  are "a really dangerous, flawed way to talk about democracy… if… all you need is 50%
  plus one person, it's very easy to sway the results."
- With more options, approval voting and a 60–70% threshold, "the community is coming
  together to say, that's the direction we want to go… It builds trust with the
  representatives. It becomes much easier to hold people to account."
- "We're just stuck in this two-party tribal bullshit situation… worse climate outcomes,
  worse domestic violence outcomes, worse immigration outcomes, worse asylum seeker
  outcomes. All of the problems… are because of this binary view." (A sweeping claim;
  quote it as his.)

**His roles and his book** (00:22:40–00:23:37)

- Global chairman of MiVote and MiVote Technologies; Director of Global and Strategic
  Innovation at Swinburne University (unrelated to MiVote); non-executive director of
  a tech company and co-founder of a "technology skunkworks" (names unclear); "five
  kids… my most important job".
- *Mythocracy*: "democracy and politics are mutually exclusive in both philosophy and
  practice… if you are engaged in politics, you will never deliver democracy. And if you
  are engaged in democracy, you have no need for politics." Check whether it was ever
  published.

**The US, China, Australia** (00:23:37–00:27:13)

- The US: "experts at calling themselves a democracy, but they're not… They're a
  republic… It costs $2 billion to become the president."
- China: "has some concerns about the way that it oppresses minorities… But… you can't
  argue [against the fact] that China has lifted more people out of poverty than any
  country in history… none of them are democracy."
- Australia: "our success is by default, not by design… our luck might be running out."
- **"I would argue that the Morrison government is basically a fascist government…
  controlled by media interests… by the Minerals Council… neoliberal… which is why
  Sydney wouldn't lock down for ages."** **Handling: omit** (see the
  register in `README.md`): a sweeping partisan label for a government, and DOD is
  nonpartisan. His underlying concerns (money in elections, enforcement) can be
  discussed without it. His evidence: "somebody… spent $87 million to try and swing the outcome of
  an election in Queensland" and the AEC "identified over 70 breaches of electoral law,
  but they don't have any power". "It's a bloody long way from a democracy."

### Brian Khuu

## Checking the blog post against the transcript

1. **"Jamie Skeller"**: the Horizon State co-founder is **Jamie Skella**. The
   transcript's spelling carried into the post.
2. **"the UK government's Entrepreneurs Programme (PEG)"**: Adam's description ("PEG…
   Department of Prime Minister and Cabinet… Innovation Council") is hard to match
   to a UK department; the names may have been misheard, or mixed up with Australian
   ones in conversation. The UK scheme that relocates overseas founders is the
   **Global Entrepreneur Programme**, run by the Department for International Trade,
   which is probably what he meant. *Handling: clarify* (done in the post).
3. **"Trials were underway with a Federal Parliament member"**: Adam says the contract
   was signed and the votes would run "over the next six months". "Planned" is more
   accurate than "underway".
4. **The 60% claim** is quoted without the approval-voting caveat (see above). Worth a
   sentence.
5. The post (reasonably) leaves out the Morrison and US/China remarks. Kept here, with
   warnings.
6. Everything else, including the Horizon State quote and the Taiwan passage, matches
   the transcript.

## Threads

1. **Organisational lessons from a democracy start-up.** "Too open" to decide, spinning
   out the tech, growing too fast, trusting the wrong partners. Rare, candid material;
   pairs with Alexar's foundation idea (2020) and Ian McBurney's capital struggles (2020).
2. **Consensus by design.** Multiple options, approval voting, 60%+ thresholds. The same
   supermajority instinct as Gruen's "10 out of 12" and "60 or 65 percent", arrived at
   from a completely different model.
3. **Representativeness.** Adam's critique of Taiwan (only those who show up) applies
   equally to MiVote's own trial votes; sortition (Gruen) is the answer he doesn't
   engage with here.
4. **Blockchain voting's arc** in DOD's archive: the Trust episode's "trust the
   mathematics" (2019), Flux's "you can't prove us wrong" (2020), and here the
   platform's creator calling the spin-out his worst decision (2021).
