# Fiji's constitutional referendum bill — watch item, nothing to write yet

**Status (2026-09-15):** Research lead surfaced this run via ConstitutionNet's
news listing. Not added to the September sync post — the actual milestones
haven't happened yet, so there's nothing to report, only something scheduled.

## What's happening

Fiji's Constitutional Review Commission (CRC) has handed its Review Report
and a Draft Constitution of the Republic of Fiji 2026 to the President, who
passed it to PM Sitiveni Rabuka. Per Fiji's Justice Minister, the CRC report
is due to be tabled at a special sitting of Parliament, and a separate
Constitution Amendment Bill and a National Referendum Bill are expected to
be considered during the regular sitting scheduled 28 September – 3 October
2026 — Fiji's first referendum on constitutional change, if it proceeds.
The Referendum Bill has drawn criticism from civil society, legal experts,
opposition parties, and Fiji's own former chief legal advisor over campaign
and information-flow restrictions in its current drafting.

## Why this isn't in the public draft this run

Nothing has actually happened yet — the CRC report tabling and both bills'
parliamentary consideration are scheduled for 28 Sep – 3 Oct, after this
run. Writing it up now would be commentary on a calendar entry, not an
event. Also a genuinely interesting angle once it resolves: a referendum
bill facing credible civil-society criticism over restricting the public's
own ability to campaign in its own referendum sits close to the
accountability framework's structural-inflexibility disqualifier (a
feedback mechanism that looks procedurally present but may be substantively
hollowed out) — worth writing carefully, not as a first-pass reaction.
