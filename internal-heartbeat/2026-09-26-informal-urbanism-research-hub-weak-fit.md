# Informal Urbanism Research Hub — weak-fit assessment

**Status: logged to the weak-fit watch list, not added to the Democracy
Landscape.** See [`orgs-not-included.md`](orgs-not-included.md)'s "Weak-fit
watch list" section.

## What prompted this

Writing up [the 2026-09-15 International Day of Democracy panel
recap](../docs/blog/posts/2026-09-26-international-day-of-democracy-recap.md),
panellist Anitra Nelson had no org-card the way Nicholas Gruen (Lateral
Economics) and Nicole Hunter (MosaicLab) did, since her institutional
affiliation — the University of Melbourne's Informal Urbanism Research Hub
(InfUr-) — has no Democracy Landscape page. The immediate fix was a plain,
un-vetted card ("Not in the Democracy Landscape" eyebrow) rather than a
Landscape entry. This note is the follow-up: is InfUr- actually a
candidate for inclusion?

## What was found

Fetched `https://infur.msd.unimelb.edu.au/` directly (not summarized) on
2026-09-26. It's hosted on the `unimelb.edu.au` subdomain — genuine
University of Melbourne affiliation, not a self-published claim. Named
co-directors: Kim Dovey, Crystal Legacy, Patrick Cobbinah, Ashraful Alam.
Anitra Nelson is one of its scholars, not a director. Active seminar
programme.

Its own self-description: "an assemblage of researchers and projects
focused on the role of urban informality in the creative production of
cities of both the Global South and North... The Hub embodies a shared
interest in how power is practised as a form of self-organisation, and in
the struggles of marginalised populations to assert their right to the
city."

## Assessment against the framework

The [Accountability
Framework](../docs/projects/accountability-framework/index.md)'s bar (via
`CLAUDE.md`'s org curation standard): "An organisation belongs in our
landscape if it is working on *how* people participate in governance —
mechanisms, structures, reforms, accountability systems." Existing
`type: research` Landscape orgs (Grattan Institute, Democracy Next,
Afrobarometer, Australia Institute) are all explicitly about democratic
governance mechanisms.

InfUr- is different in kind: it's urban-studies/critical-geography
research on informal settlements and spatial practice. "Self-organisation"
and "power" are named directly in its own description, which is genuinely
adjacent to governance theory — but its centre of gravity is urban
planning and the built environment, not designing or reforming
participation mechanisms. No disqualifier applies either (nothing
hypocritical, bad-faith, or structurally inflexible here) — it's simply
not obviously the kind of org the Landscape tracks. A weak fit, not a
zero fit.

## Why log it rather than just say no

Per the new "Weak-fit watch list" section this entry seeded: DOD has an
actual tie to InfUr- now (Anitra Nelson, a real interaction, not a
stranger's org read cold). The strength-of-weak-ties bet is that this kind
of loose, already-existing connection is more likely than a cold one to
surface something that later does clear the bar — a future InfUr- project
that's explicitly about informal/self-organised governance structures, or
a co-authored piece that makes the governance-mechanism angle explicit.
Filing it as a flat "no" would lose that thread; the watch list keeps it
live without pretending it's a settled inclusion.

## Open question for a future pass

If InfUr- publishes work that more explicitly frames informal
self-organisation as a governance mechanism (rather than an urban-planning
lens on it), that's the trigger to re-run this assessment.
