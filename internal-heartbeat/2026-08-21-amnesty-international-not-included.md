# Amnesty International (Australia) — assessed, not included

## What was asked

While evaluating Digital Rights Watch (DRW) and Electronic Frontiers
Australia (EFA) for the Democracy Landscape, a DOD member asked whether
Amnesty International — named by DRW as one of its founding/coalition
partners — should be added too.

## What was found

DRW (founded 2016) and EFA (founded 1994) both centre their work on
*mechanisms of oversight over state and corporate power in the digital
domain*: EFA's 2025 submission on the Freedom of Information Amendment
Bill, its OAIC complaints over facial recognition, and its "Not My Debt"
campaign against the unlawful Robodebt automated debt-recovery scheme;
DRW's advocacy for "transparency, accountability and oversight over the
use of data" and its AI Harms Register. That's governance-mechanism work
in the sense the [Accountability
Framework](../docs/projects/accountability-framework/index.md) asks for —
not just whether a violation happened, but whether there's a structural
check on the power that could cause one.

Amnesty International Australia's own framing of its work is different in
kind: "we search out the facts, expose what's happening and rally people
together to pressure governments" ([About Amnesty International
Australia](https://www.amnesty.org.au/about/)). Its activity types —
Urgent Action-style letter-writing (Write for Rights), petitions,
targeted campaigns on specific issues (Indigenous youth justice, refugee
rights, climate justice, Palestinian rights, death penalty abolition),
research reports documenting abuses — are centred on *exposure and
pressure* around rights violations, not on designing, reforming, or
providing oversight of governance mechanisms themselves. It does make
legislative submissions (e.g. on Human Rights Act implementation), but
that's one activity among many inside a mandate whose centre of gravity
is documentation-and-campaigning, not institutional-mechanism work.

## The test applied

This is the same distinction the [Accountability
Framework](../docs/projects/accountability-framework/index.md) already
draws — "DOD is not a human rights observatory... An organisation belongs
in our landscape if it is working on *how* people participate in
governance — mechanisms, structures, reforms, accountability systems —
not just whether rights are being violated" — but the framework's only
worked example of a human-rights-shaped org that still qualifies
(Memorial, in the Russia case study) doesn't spell out the test in a
reusable, non-Russia-specific form. The operational version used here:

> Is the org's centre of gravity *oversight/reform of a governance
> mechanism*, or *documentation-and-campaigning about violations*?

EFA and FOI/surveillance-oversight work → the former. Amnesty's core
mandate → the latter, even though it does some legislative-submission
work at the margins.

## Verdict

**Not included**, on the current framework as written. This isn't a
verdict on Amnesty's legitimacy or importance — DOD is explicitly not a
human rights observatory by design, and this is the kind of org that
line exists to route around, not a judgment that the work doesn't matter.
