# Research leads

A holding pen for things worth DOD's attention that **aren't ready to become
anything yet**: a video, paper, article or talk someone shared that might one
day feed a blog post, a concept page, a social-media share or a reading list,
but that nobody has the review capacity to turn into DOD's public word right
now.

The motivating case (2026-10-02): a video essay worth sharing, but a blog post
on *non-DOD* material needs a human reviewer to own it (see CLAUDE.md's Blog
posts section), and DOD doesn't yet have enough reviewers for that. Rather than
publish it unreviewed, or lose the link in a chat log, it's parked here.

Same visibility rules as the rest of `internal-heartbeat/`: unpublished, not
private. The repo is public, so nothing goes here that would matter if read.

## What a lead is for

A lead is a pointer plus enough context that someone picking it up later
doesn't have to re-watch or re-read the source just to decide whether it's
worth their time. Likely destinations, roughly in order of how little review
they need:

- **Social media / content discovery** — a share with a one-line framing.
- **Reading/watch list** — if DOD ever grows one.
- **Concept page link-out** — concept pages are discovery aids that point
  outward, so a strong source can be linked from the relevant concept.
- **Blog post** — needs a human author per CLAUDE.md; a lead file is a head
  start on the research, not a draft.

## Conventions

- One file per lead: `YYYY-MM-DD-short-slug.md` (date the lead was logged).
- Open with a status line: `Status: open` | `shared (<where, link>)` |
  `promoted (<link to what shipped>)` | `dropped (<why>)`. Update it when the
  lead goes somewhere, so the folder doesn't read as a pile of unresolved
  questions forever.
- Record: the link, title, creator, format/length, who shared it, a short
  summary in our own words, why it's relevant to DOD, possible destinations,
  and open questions (accuracy, framing, nonpartisanship concerns).
- **Summarise, don't archive.** Don't paste full transcripts or article text
  into the repo — it's someone else's work in a public repo. A few short
  quoted phrases are fine; the link is the source of truth.
- Note anything a reviewer should check before it goes out under DOD's name
  (claims made by the source that we haven't verified, sensitive framing).
