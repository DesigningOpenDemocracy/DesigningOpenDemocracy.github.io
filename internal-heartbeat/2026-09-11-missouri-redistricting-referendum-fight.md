# Missouri redistricting referendum fight — research note, not yet public

**Status:** Research lead. Not promoted to a public heartbeat item this run —
see reasoning below. Revisit on a future run once the legal sequence has
actually settled; as of this writing it flipped three times in eight days.

## What's happening

Missouri's Republican-controlled legislature passed a new mid-decade
congressional map (House Bill 1) in 2025, widely reported as intended to
flip a Democratic-held U.S. House seat (Kansas City) to Republican — part of
a broader multi-state mid-decade redistricting push connected to the Trump
administration's efforts to protect its House majority ahead of the 2026
midterms.

Missouri has a constitutional citizen referendum mechanism: opponents of HB1
gathered 300,000+ signatures to force a public vote on the map, which under
state law suspends the challenged law until voters decide. On 3 September
2026 the Missouri Supreme Court ruled unanimously that the referendum
petition was valid and timely, holding that HB1 "is not the law and has
never been the law" until voters decide it in November, and that the map
used in the August primary could not carry into the general election.[^msc]

On 8 September 2026, U.S. District Chief Judge Stephen Clark (a Trump
appointee) issued a federal temporary restraining order requiring Missouri
to use the HB1 map for the November general election anyway, reasoning that
changing district lines after primary candidates had already been nominated
under HB1 could violate candidates'/voters' federal constitutional
rights.[^clark]

On 10 September 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court granted an emergency
application from a political action committee (People Not Politicians) to
stay Clark's TRO — reviving the Missouri Supreme Court's referendum-blocks-
the-map ruling, at least for now.[^scotus]

## Why this is a live governance-design story, not just US partisan noise

This isn't "who wins the seat" — it's a structural accountability-mechanism
question squarely in the framework's territory: does a citizen referendum
power (a direct-democracy check the state constitution grants) actually
bind a legislature's map, or can it be overridden by a federal court acting
on a theory about candidate/ballot continuity? That's a live test of
whether a feedback/correction mechanism to power (the referendum) is real
or can be routed around procedurally — exactly the kind of question Step 5
asks this brief to watch for, and exactly the kind of thing the
accountability framework's "structural inflexibility" disqualifier is built
to evaluate (see `docs/projects/accountability-framework/index.md`, though
note that disqualifier is written for assessing a governance *system*, not
adjudicating a live single-country court fight — applying it here would be
new ground, not a mechanical lookup).

## Why this isn't in the public draft this run

1. **Still moving.** Three reversals in eight days (Missouri Supreme Court →
   federal TRO → SCOTUS stay) as of 10 September. Writing this up now risks
   the post being stale or wrong within days if there's a fourth turn — the
   TRO/stay posture reported above is itself provisional, pending further
   proceedings.
2. **Politically loaded, not just structurally interesting.** The map is
   explicitly tied to the sitting US administration's stated national
   strategy, and every outlet covering it (Missouri Independent, Democracy
   Docket, PBS, STLPR) has an identifiable lean on redistricting fights of
   this kind. Getting the neutral, structural framing right — say what the
   referendum-vs-court-override tension means for accountability mechanisms,
   without reading as "Team A good, Team B bad" — needs more care and a
   later, stabler news cycle than a single research pass can responsibly
   give it. HEARTBEAT.md's Voice section allows structural-governance
   commentary even when it touches a party in government, but this item's
   volatility makes it a bad candidate for a single-pass drafting job.
3. **No landscape org is actually the subject.** This doesn't connect to a
   tracked org's own page (unlike the Niger item, which at least maps onto
   Memorial/FBK-adjacent accountability-sink concepts already in the
   landscape) — it would be commentary from a standing start.
