# Forensic Architecture — assessed, not included

## What was asked

A DOD member recalled that Forensic Architecture had been considered and
rejected for the Democracy Landscape previously. No written record of
that earlier discussion was found in this repo (`internal-heartbeat/` or
`docs/`) — this entry is the first time the reasoning has been captured
in writing, recorded now while the recollection is fresh, per the same
conversation that produced the Amnesty International entry above.

## What was found

Forensic Architecture is a research agency founded in 2010 at Goldsmiths,
University of London, directed by architect Eyal Weizman. Per its own
framing (via [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forensic_Architecture)),
it practises "counter-forensics" — applying forensic investigative
methodology to scrutinise *state* actions, using architectural and
open-source spatial-analysis techniques, in cases where it "lacks the
privileges of the state's forensic process — access to crime scenes,
resources, and the power to set the rules of evidence."

Its output is entirely evidentiary: investigative reports submitted to
international prosecutors and courts, interactive data-visualisation
platforms, video/multimedia evidence presentations, museum and gallery
exhibitions, and legal submissions to judicial bodies. Recent
investigations span armed conflict in Gaza, Syria's Saydnaya Prison,
Mediterranean migration deaths, and extrajudicial killings; it received
the Right Livelihood Award in 2024.

## The test applied

Same operational test used for Amnesty (see that entry and the
[Accountability Framework](../docs/projects/accountability-framework/index.md)):
*is the org's centre of gravity oversight/reform of a governance
mechanism, or documentation-and-campaigning about violations?*

Forensic Architecture is a cleaner case than Amnesty, not a closer call.
It has no legislative-advocacy or mechanism-design activity to weigh —
its work is entirely the production of rigorous, technically
sophisticated *evidence* for use by mechanisms that already exist (courts,
UN inquiry bodies, prosecutors). That's a stronger and more specialised
form of documentation than Amnesty's public campaigning, but it's still
documentation feeding an external accountability process, not the
organisation itself designing, reforming, or overseeing one.

## Verdict

**Not included.** Tagged `human-rights-observatory` / `documentation-only`
— the purest instance of that category logged here so far. As with
Amnesty, this isn't a judgment on the value or rigor of the work — DOD's
explicit "not a human rights observatory" scope line exists precisely to
route around organisations doing exactly this kind of investigation, however
well done.
